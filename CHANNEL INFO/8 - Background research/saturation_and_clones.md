# Research: saturation_and_clones

Collected 5 Oct 2026 by an independent research agent. Confidence labels are the agent's own.

## Headline
Stick-figure prehistory is saturated and the near-clone window is closed for new entrants: of eight pattern channels created since 1 July, one broke out, and it uploaded about once a day. Ink Explainer is itself a follower (Zenn's April hit and Axen's May hit came first), and the X tutorial is one day old, so another cohort is coming. Generic "How did humans invent X" topics are already farmed daily at low views; named-era everyday life and character-led shows are the least contested. What reads as different in a thumbnail is a non-stickman character, a different palette, and a title that does not open with "Ancient Humans".

## Implications
1. Do not launch a prehistory near-clone (white stick figure plus 'What/How Did Ancient Humans...'). The measured outcome for entrants since July is under 2K views per video, title copies cap near 10K, and tutorials from 23 Sep and 4 Oct will add another cohort within weeks.
2. Concept C needs repositioning before piloting. Its territory (things and systems humans invented) is already posted near-daily by Neon Rush, Explain In Paint, Stickly and Deep Epoch at 1K-15K views. Avoid 'How/Why/When Did Humans Invent X' titles; anchor each episode in one named case, place and date, with the character carrying it.
3. Concept A is the least overlapped. The farms stay generic ('ancient humans', 'humans invented'); specific documented cases such as the ice trade or siege logistics are not what they produce. Expect them to move into Rome, Egypt and medieval next, so the moat has to be the character and research depth, not the era.
4. For concept B, avoid 'The ___ Effect' titles and stick figures: Zenn owns that pairing at 194K subscribers, and its recent behavior uploads mostly sit at 14K-38K. Behavior is viable but hit-driven.
5. Plan a slow cadence with higher craft, roughly one video every 7-14 days. In this niche daily uploaders get 5K-13K medians, while the slow-cadence reference channel has a median near 293K.
6. Make the difference readable at thumbnail size: a coloured, non-stickman recurring character (Astra's Mara, Nib and Mox silhouettes all qualify), a non-savanna palette, and no title that opens with 'Ancient Humans'. This is inferred from titles, a thumbnail job brief and tool templates, since I could not view thumbnails.
7. Do not use the preset stickman styles in Higgsfield or Vsub, or the tutorial's 'feed a competitor transcript to Claude' step. Both reproduce the look and script skeleton the wave shares, and templated near-duplicates are what YouTube's inauthentic-content policy targets.
8. Drop the 10M video and the $98K/month figure as planning inputs. The only revenue figure tied to Ink Explainer is an unverified agency claim of $17.8K in its best 28 days; 4 of 35 sampled channels have a video over 1M, and the best recent entrant reached 9K subscribers after about 93 uploads.
9. Treat visible sourcing as a differentiator and a safety margin. Astra's evidence / reconstruction / interpretation split is worth keeping: the genre's biggest hits lean on the 15-20 hour work-week claim, which I recall being contested in anthropology (from memory, not checked).

## Gaps
- I could not view thumbnails, video frames or comments (no browser; YouTube pages consent-walled). Visual similarity between channels and comment-level sentiment are inferred, not observed, and not every one of the 35 channels is confirmed to be stick-figure in style.
- The X post itself returned HTTP 402. Its date comes from decoding the post ID; its text, engagement and replies are unread, and at one day old its downstream effect cannot be measured yet.
- Axen's current subscriber and view counts were not fetched (channel ID not resolved). Its figures are from OutlierKit dated 23 Jul 2026.
- The true number of copycat channels is unknown. My sample comes from search engines and OutlierKit 'similar channel' lists, which favours winners in the mid tier and exact-title copies in the tail.
- About 29 named channels were not checked, including Kokishin, Humanly Odd, Pastly, Knapp, inkly, StickStory, Before Civilization, Chill Scientist Explains and Bones and Minds.
- OutlierKit pages are partly machine-written (they describe stick-figure channels as 'stock footage'). I used them for numbers and capture dates only; their dates range from 23 Jul to 26 Sep, so some figures are stale.
- The shared-operator reading of Mack, Explain In Paint and Stickly rests on identical upload times and overlapping titles; mutual scraping would produce the same pattern.
- The YT Wealth Media claim to have built Ink Explainer is self-reported marketing with an inconsistent timeline; I found no independent confirmation.
- TikTok and Reddit tutorial activity was not checked, and I found no Reddit threads about this niche at all, which may reflect search coverage rather than absence.
- Medieval doodle clones: my one search surfaced sleep-documentary and long-form channels rather than stick-figure ones. That is weak evidence the space is open.
- Older lineage figures (Sam O'Nella, History Matters, OverSimplified, Casually Explained) come from search snippets, not pages I fetched. The remark that the 15-20 hour work-week claim is contested is from memory.
