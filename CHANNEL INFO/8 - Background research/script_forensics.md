# Research: script_forensics

Collected 5 Oct 2026 by an independent research agent. Confidence labels are the agent's own.

## Headline
Astra's Section 2 is accurate but too coarse to explain the hit. All four scripts share the same generic mechanics; the 10.5M video differs by putting the viewer's own lost time at stake, naming a culprit and holding one question open for about eight minutes. That is still not a formula: the creator reused the same opening beat for beat on 27 September and has 62K views after a week. All Day's showpiece finds are accurate, but its core thesis (a 17-hour week on three continents, two-phase sleep, healthier Middle East skeletons) is simplified to misleading, and a bibliography alone will not make rigor visible to viewers.

### F8 [high] All Day's central number is simplified to the point of misleading. About 17 hours is food-getting only, and the claim that Hadza, Ache and Martu all came back at 15 to 20 hours is not supported.
Lee's broader accounting, adding tool-making and housework, gives 44.5 hours a week for men and 40.1 for women. Ache men spent about seven hours a day on subsistence (Hill et al. 1985). The description cites nothing for Ache or Martu. Better support exists but is unused: Dyble 2019 found Agta foragers at about 20 hours versus 30 for farming. The channel's Pleasure video says 4 to 6 hours a day.

### F9 [medium] The other pillars are mixed. The showpiece finds are accurate, but two-phase sleep, the two Middle East skeletons and walking-pace persistence hunting as normal hunting are weaker than presented.

## Implications
1. Concept choice: the lever with evidence behind it is a premise where the viewer's own daily life is at stake and the answer is contrarian. Of Astra's pilots, C's clock episode is closest to that and furthest from the prehistoric frame. A's pilots are spectator catalogs like Rain and Winter and need an angle on what the viewer still pays or does today. B must never use named-effect or definitional titles, which is IKEA's failure mode.
2. Do not treat any script template as the asset. The creator's own reuse of the All Day opening has not repeated the result, and the catalog is one outlier in 17. Budget for several premise and packaging tests, as Astra recommends.
3. Pilot script spec: within about 65 seconds deliver viewer stakes, the viewer's voiced objection and two or three explicit questions, with one held until the final quarter. Change section every 85 to 100 seconds. Include a prediction-then-reversal beat; Astra's Mox steps 2 and 3 already fit this if the viewer's guess is invited too. End on a callback.
4. Pace: do not copy 208 to 245 words per minute. For a character-led show that leaves room for visual jokes, I would plan roughly 170 to 190 wpm, about 1,400 to 1,900 words for 8 to 10 minutes. This is my recommendation, not a measured optimum.
5. Research workflow: build the claim sheet with page-level citations, tag each line as evidence, reconstruction or interpretation, and write the strongest counter-evidence into the script as the complication. Add a pre-publish check of every spoken number against the description; the Rain slip shows why.
6. Do not use the transcript-to-similar-script workflow from the X tutorial. It would import the same errors (15 to 20 hours on three continents, two-phase sleep) and the same fingerprint phrases, and search results already show near-identical titles from other channels.
7. Humor is an open lane. The reference narration is nearly joke-free, so a character with a real comic point of view (Mox or Nib) is a genuine differentiator, provided the jokes are written into the script and not left to the art.
8. Packaging hygiene: write creator-authored chapters and an own-words production note. The reference relies on auto-chapters and YouTube's platform label, and its bulleted sources layout is part of its look.
9. Avoid list for any concept: titles beginning What or How Did Ancient Humans; a comfort-now opening followed by a rewind of N years; nobody-tells-you bridges; a you-versus-they sermon ending; the fire-created-culture conclusion; the Wiessner, Sibudu, Hohle Fels and Blombos cluster as default evidence.

## Checks of Astra 6 claims
- [confirmed] Four complete caption texts (All Day, Rain, Winter, IKEA) were accessible and compared. -- All four prepublish pages returned full text. They are YouTube auto-captions, so numbers and names can be mis-transcribed.
- [confirmed] Structure table: openings, development and payoffs for the four episodes. -- Each row matches the transcript. It omits All Day's skeleton guessing game and its stacked questions, which are the most distinctive beats.
- [partly] Repeatable pattern: situation, expectation, complication, explanation, implication, return to the opening. -- Fits All Day and IKEA. Rain and Winter are threat lists with problem-solution chains; Rain returns to its title question, not its opening scene.
- [confirmed] Direct address puts the viewer inside the scene. -- Measured at 2.5 to 6.9 you/your per 100 words. It does not discriminate: the weakest video (IKEA) has the highest rate.
- [partly] Modern comparisons add humor. -- They mostly add contrast and translation. I count only two to four light quips per script and almost none in Winter; visuals not assessed.
- [confirmed] One solved problem often exposes the next. -- Strong in Winter and Rain. All Day instead moves by time of day and by answering anticipated doubts.
- [partly] The All Day page has a research bibliography, chapters and a visible AI disclosure. -- Bibliography exists (11 entries, five DOIs). Chapters are YouTube auto-chapters. The disclosure is YouTube's altered-or-generated label; the description only credits animation to the channel.
- [confirmed] All Day showed 10,407,278 views on the watch page. -- I read 10,544,579 on 5 October, consistent with one more day of growth.
- [confirmed] These are plausible engagement mechanisms, not measured retention results. -- Correct caution. The creator's own reuse of the hook (Predators, 62K in 7 days) shows the mechanics are not sufficient.
- [confirmed] One older psychology upload (IKEA) is insufficient evidence to reject behavior as a niche. -- IKEA also differs in script terms: definitional title, 4:09 length, zero questions, generic SEO description, uploaded 28 April 2026 on a new channel.
- [confirmed] The caption comparison is not independent verification of factual claims; not every historical claim was verified. -- My check finds All Day's work-hours, sleep and skeleton claims overstated. Astra's evidence, reconstruction, interpretation rule is the right safeguard.

## Gaps
- No retention or click-through data. Replay peaks are not retention, and two of Winter's three peaks sit near chapter starts, so they may reflect chapter seeking.
- Transcripts are auto-captions. Rain's 5,200-year figure could be a caption error rather than a narration error. Beat timestamps are estimated from word position at average pace, except where YouTube chapter times were available.
- Transcript-only analysis: on-screen text, visual jokes and image pacing were not assessed.
- Not verified in primary sources: Suzman's six hours of daily social time (not found in searches), Martu work hours, and Marlowe's Hadza figures (my recollection is about 4 hours a day for women and 6 for men foraging, medium confidence). From memory with high confidence: Lee's diary covered four weeks in mid-1964, and Ju/'hoansi hunters mainly used poisoned arrows.
- The Eshed Levant figures and the Hill 1985 Ache figure came from search-result summaries of the papers, not the papers themselves. Dyble 2019 was read via Cambridge press coverage because nature.com redirected to a login.
- All four watch pages serve a View corrections card from the channel. I could not retrieve any correction text, and it appears even on IKEA, so I cannot tell whether real corrections were issued.
- Only four of 17 scripts were read in full, plus openings and endings of three more. The hit-versus-rest comparison is one outlier, confounded by thumbnail, upload timing, channel age and auto-dubbed audio in seven languages.
- Near-identical titles and similarly named channels were seen only as search-result listings and were not opened.
