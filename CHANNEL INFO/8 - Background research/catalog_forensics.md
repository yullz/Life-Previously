# Research: catalog_forensics

Collected 5 Oct 2026 by an independent research agent. Confidence labels are the agent's own.

## Headline
The 10M hit did not raise Ink Explainer's baseline: post-hit uploads (223K at 31 days, 62K at 7.7 days, 7K in 14 hours at 113K subs) match or trail pre-hit ones, and the 13 older videos still earn about 40K/day combined, as before. Performance tracks topic and packaging: videos titled with "Ancient Humans" have a median near 818K versus 154K for the rest, and the hit itself remade a question another channel (Axen) had already taken viral by June. Astra's Section 3 arithmetic checks out, but its reading is too generous to behavior and off-promise topics; none of concepts A, B or C keeps the ingredient the data supports.

## Implications
1. Do not read Ink Explainer's 113K subs or 10M hit as support for concepts A, B or C. The only thing its data supports is 'a modern everyday predicament dropped into deep prehistory'. Concept C's pilots (receipts, clocks, queues) and concept A's (ice trade, sieges) sit closest to Guns (45K); concept B's bias-style pilots sit closest to IKEA (16K). If the user wants B or C, the case must come from other reference channels, not this one.
2. Drop Astra's plan to make one history-heavy and one behavior-heavy pilot under one brand. On this channel, mixing topic families cost 5-18x in views; pick one audience promise and keep every early episode inside it.
3. Do not budget on a subscriber 'baseline'. Even at 113K subs each upload is judged alone (62K in a week). For a new channel, plan on 12-15 episodes inside one promise before judging; Ink's outlier was upload 14 and one video is 59% of all views.
4. Do not copy the 'Ancient Humans' slate. Axen, the originator, now gets 3K-46K per video; at least seven other channels reuse the 'all day/jobs' title; same-name clones get under 2K. By my decode of the X status ID the clone tutorial was posted around 4 Oct 2026, so more copies are coming.
5. The transferable mechanism is: take a question already proven viral elsewhere, reframe it around the viewer's own life ('NO JOBS'), and execute it better. Build topic selection around checking adjacent channels' outliers first, then test three title and thumbnail pairs per episode; wording alone ('Early' versus 'Ancient', label-style titles) separated 39K from 800K+ here.
6. Plan for 9-12 minute episodes rather than Astra's 6-9 minute target, and a realistic solo cadence of one video every 10-14 days. The reference channel, possibly agency-run, itself slowed from every 10 days to every 14-23 days as videos lengthened.
7. Treat any revenue or 'system' claims around this channel as marketing. The agency case study's timeline is impossible for a six-month-old channel, and nothing public verifies who produces the videos or with which tools.

- [partly] Videos range roughly 4 to 15 minutes, averaging about 9:20 -- Mean for 16 is 9:21 (9:40 with 17). It hides the drift from about 7:20 (first eight) to about 11:44 (last nine), which undercuts Astra's 6-9 minute target.

- [partly] IKEA Effect: one older psychology upload is insufficient evidence to reject behavior as a niche -- True as narrowly worded, but too generous. There are two behavior uploads (IKEA 16K, Life Speeds Up 154K) plus an animal-cognition one (293K), all below the prehistory median, and the owner dropped the category after May. Ink offers no positive evidence for behavior; IKEA does count against label-style bias titles.

- [partly] Public data does not show which factor produced a hit -- Public data does show the hit's question was already viral on Axen (Upworthy feature 4 June; 2.9M by 23 July) before Ink remade it on 21 Aug. Astra missed this, and it bears on both the cause of the hit and the originality framing.
- [confirmed] The catalog currently emphasizes prehistoric life, though the channel describes itself as history, behavior and everyday science -- Understated: 10 of the 11 uploads after 26 May are prehistory, and the one exception (Guns) is the worst performer since May.
- [partly] Production team and toolchain not identified -- Still unverified, but an automation agency (YT Wealth Media) publicly claims to have designed the channel's system. Its case study contains an impossible timeline, so treat it as a claim only.

## Gaps
- No daily view or subscriber curve: Social Blade, vidIQ and HypeAuditor returned 403, ViewStats is JavaScript-only, Playboard video pages were blocked, and the Wayback availability API returned no snapshots. The hit's takeoff date is inferred from a handful of dated third-party snapshots.
- First-week views for pre-hit uploads could not be recovered. The baseline conclusion rests on the Terrifying/Pleasure matched pair at 1-2 months, current run-rates, and the Axen control, not on like-for-like first-week numbers.
- The two key early snapshots are dated by inference from video counts: channelslike (9 videos, so about 23 Jun-3 Jul) and the earlier OutlierKit figures (13 videos, so 7-21 Aug), the latter seen only in a search-index summary, not on the live page.
- The 30 Sep prepublish figures may have been cached at slightly different times per video, so the 30 Sep to 5 Oct run-rates are approximate.
- Exact timestamps and exact view counts for the two oldest videos (27 and 28 Apr) come only from prepublish and the orchestrator's rounded snapshot.
- Axen's hit: exact publish date (before 4 June 2026) and current view count were not fetched.
- Sister channel identity is unknown; YT Wealth Media's involvement is self-claimed and unverified. Whether 'Ink Explainer Official' is affiliated was not verifiable, though its numbers do not match the claimed sister channel.
- Thumbnail change history could not be checked. The Only Species title change is probable, not proven, because the orchestrator's 5 Oct snapshot shows the short title.
- Channel country is unknown (NoxInfluencer shows Germany without supporting data). CTR, retention and traffic sources are private.
- The X tutorial date (about 4 Oct 2026) is my own decode of the status ID, not something read from the post.
