# Research: platform_policy_risk

Collected 5 Oct 2026 by an independent research agent. Confidence labels are the agent's own.

## Headline
YouTube does not ban AI-assisted illustrated explainers, AI narration or faceless channels. What it refuses to pay for (official policy page, read 5 Oct 2026) is templated, interchangeable content, now including content that "mimics existing formats or stories" until videos feel interchangeable, and it judges the channel as a whole. The X-tutorial pipeline (competitor transcript to similar script, stock AI voice, AI image slideshow) is the profile hit by the 2025-2026 enforcement, so I rate it HIGH risk. Own voice or own cloned voice, a human-drawn character and independently researched scripts is LOW-MEDIUM; the residual risk is the documented false-positive sweep of legitimate faceless channels. Astra's report omits this whole layer, plus AI-character copyright, EU AI Act and Bulgarian tax set-up.

## Findings

### F1 [high] (a) Official rule: AI use is allowed. What makes AI-assisted content ineligible for the Partner Program is template or mass production with no creator perspective, judged across the whole channel.
Fetched support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392 (5 Oct 2026). Renamed 15 Jul 2025. Non-monetizable examples: AI content from 'generic or unoriginal templates', templated storylines, image slideshows with minimal narrative. Explicitly acceptable: AI to visualize a unique character you invented, edit scripts, generate unique backgrounds, if the result shows your 'creative vision'. Reviewers check main theme, most-viewed and newest videos, metadata, About section; policy applies channel-wide.
- source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en

### F2 [high] (a) Mid-2026 update: the policy now names format mimicry directly, and bars monetization for AI personas giving advice on sensitive topics. Both matter for a show whose structure derives from Ink Explainer and for a behavior channel.
Same official page now has three headings: generic/repetitive content, 'unsatisfying or off-putting content', and AI personas on sensitive topics (health, legal, financial, political). The second covers content that 'mimics existing formats or stories' until videos 'feel interchangeable'; building on a popular format with your own spin is allowed. Dexerto (15 Jul 2026) reports YouTube framed it as clarification. The page carries no dated note for this change.

### F5 [high] (c) The 'altered or synthetic content' disclosure is not required for obviously stylized illustration, AI-assisted scripts or thumbnails, or a clone of your own voice. If you disclose anyway, an animated video shows the label only in the expanded description.
Fetched support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491: disclosure needed for realistic altered/synthetic content and AI music; not needed for clearly unrealistic animation, AI-assisted scripts/thumbnails/ideas, or 'cloning one's own voice'. Stock AI narration is listed in neither group. YouTube blog (27 May 2026): photorealistic content gets a label under the player; animated content only in expanded description; undisclosed photorealistic AI auto-labelled, appealable; label alone doesn't change recommendations or earnings.

### F6 [medium] (d) AI narration is not prohibited and carries no official monetization or recommendation penalty. The risk is indirect: a stock synthetic voice is part of the profile reviewers and viewers associate with mass production. ElevenLabs output is commercially usable only on paid plans.

### F7 [high] (e) US: a character produced only by prompting is not copyrightable. A character you draw yourself is, and that protection carries into AI-assisted scenes to the extent your drawing remains perceptible in them.
Copyright Office Part 2 report (29 Jan 2025, via Jones Day summary): prompts alone insufficient; protected are human-authored inputs perceptible in the output (e.g. your own drawing), creative modifications, and selection/arrangement; case by case. Thaler: D.C. Circuit March 2025, Supreme Court declined review 2 Mar 2026 (Copyright Lately). Allen v. Perlmutter (624 prompts) reportedly undecided as of Aug 2026.
- source: https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/02/copyrightability-of-ai-outputs-us-copyright-office-analyzes-human-authorship-requirement
- source: https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf
- source: https://copyrightlately.com/thaler-is-dead-ai-copyright-questions/

### F8 [medium] (e) EU: same direction as the US, with no dedicated statute. Protection follows human creative choices; purely AI-generated output is treated as unprotected. A trade mark on the show and character name is the practical complement.
European Parliament resolution of 10 Mar 2026 (460-71-88 per search results; non-binding) says content 'entirely generated by AI without human creative input' should not qualify (Jacobacci summary). The EU case-law standard of the author's own intellectual creation, and the point that trade marks need no human author, are from memory (medium confidence). Bulgarian national copyright law was not checked.
- source: https://www.jacobacci-law.com/news-and-publications/european-parliament-adopts-resolution-on-copyright-and-generative-ai-key-implications-for-ai-developers-and-rights-holders-en
- source: https://www.ddg.fr/actualite/copyright-and-generative-artificial-intelligence-the-european-parliament-resolution-of-10-march-2026

### F9 [medium] (f) Borrowing the general explainer structure is legally safe. Borrowing a specific video's script via its transcript, or the reference channel's look, is where copyright, YPP and impersonation risk sit. Astra's three concepts are far enough from Ink Explainer if scripts are independently researched.
Idea/expression rule (general copyright principle, from memory, high confidence): structure and question formats are free; script text, drawings, thumbnails are not. YouTube's impersonation policy (fetched, answer/2801947) bans copying another channel's profile picture, banner or 'specific color schemes' and can remove the channel. The X tutorial's transcript-to-'similar script' step risks derivative-work claims plus the 'mimics existing formats' rule. No explainer-niche clone takedown case found.
- source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801947?hl=en
- source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en

### F10 [medium] (g) Bulgaria: the Partner Program is available. US withholding on US-viewer earnings falls to 5% with a W-8BEN treaty claim, versus 30% without a treaty claim or up to 24% of worldwide earnings with no form. Bulgarian VAT registration is reportedly needed before the first Google payment.
Bulgaria is on YouTube's YPP country list (answer/7101720). Google's tax page (answer/10391362): all monetizing creators submit tax info; withholding applies only to US-viewer earnings; 30% without treaty; up to 24% of worldwide earnings if no form. US-Bulgaria treaty Art. 12 caps royalties at 5% (Senate Exec. Rpt. 110-16). Art. 97a VAT registration before first Google Ireland payment and 10% tax come from one advisory firm (Innovires).
- source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/7101720?hl=en
- source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362?hl=en
- source: https://www.congress.gov/110/crpt/erpt16/CRPT-110erpt16.pdf
- source: https://www.innovires.com/en/blog/oblagane-influenseri-youtube-tiktok-instagram-dac7.html

### F11 [medium] (g) EU AI Act Article 50 has applied since 2 Aug 2026, but a stylized cartoon explainer is not a deepfake, so the creator has no mandatory labelling duty for the visuals. A monetizing creator does count as a deployer, so realistic depictions of real people, places or events would trigger disclosure.
Commission FAQ (fetched): Art. 50 applies from 2 Aug 2026; provider marking grace until 2 Dec 2026. Deepfake needs resemblance to existing persons/places/events plus false appearance of authenticity; evidently creative works get lighter disclosure. People earning regularly count as deployers. AI text on public-interest matters is exempt after substantive human review. Fines up to EUR 15M or 3%. Code of Practice published 10 Jun 2026, voluntary.
- source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act
- source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content
- source: https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/

### F12 [medium] Risk rating (my judgment from the above). Setup 1, fully AI pipeline with stock AI voice: HIGH. It matches the named non-monetizable patterns, has no copyright in its character or frames, and uses a voice heard on many channels. Setup 2, own or own-cloned voice plus human-designed character with AI-assisted illustration: LOW-MEDIUM. It matches YouTube's own 'acceptable' examples; the residual risk is a false-positive faceless-channel sweep.
Checklist: (1) hand-draw or commission the character sheet, keep dated source files; (2) own voice or own clone on a paid plan; (3) independent research, sources in description; (4) never derive scripts from another channel's transcript; (5) each episode distinct, modest cadence; (6) About section explains your process; (7) voluntary AI note in description; (8) W-8BEN, Bulgarian VAT/tax set-up; (9) trade mark the show name; (10) keep drafts for appeals.

## Implications
1. Do not run the X-tutorial pipeline as shown. Its first step (take a popular video's transcript and ask for a similar script) is the one step that creates copyright, reused-content and 'mimics existing formats' exposure at once. Start every script from your own question and source list.
2. Voice: record yourself, or clone your own voice on a paid ElevenLabs plan. YouTube lists own-voice cloning as needing no disclosure, and it removes the stock-voice signal. A stock library voice is allowed but is the weakest choice for both review and audience.
3. Character: for whichever concept wins (Mara, Nib or Mox), draw the model sheet by hand or commission it with a written rights assignment before any AI generation, then use it as the reference input. A prompt-only character has no US copyright and anyone could reuse it.
4. Concept choice: B (behavior) carries one extra policy constraint. Keep the character a fictional clerk explaining studies, never an AI 'expert' giving health, financial or legal advice, which is a named non-monetizable category. A (history) and C (bridge) have no topic-specific policy flag.
5. Format: static AI image slideshows are a named risk only when narrative is thin. Budget for real explanatory motion, diagrams and character action, and make the first 5-10 uploads visibly different from each other, because reviewers judge newest and most-viewed videos, metadata and the About section together.
6. Packaging: do not echo Ink Explainer's yellow outlined lettering, character proportions or 'Disturbing/TERRIFYING' title pattern. The impersonation policy covers look-and-feel copying, and shock-led formula is what the 'unsatisfying or off-putting' category describes.
7. Disclosure: no toggle is required for stylized illustration. Add a one-line voluntary note in the description about AI-assisted illustration and voice; it costs nothing (YouTube says a label alone does not affect recommendations or earnings) and covers the EU position.
8. Plan for a false-positive demonetization. Keep dated script drafts, sketches, project files and recording sessions as an evidence pack, write your process into the About section, and do not make AdSense the only revenue line in year one.
9. Before the first payout, file the W-8BEN with the 5% treaty claim and have a Bulgarian accountant confirm the VAT registration and income-tax treatment. The cost of skipping the form is up to 24% of worldwide earnings.
10. Register the final show name as a trade mark once chosen; Astra notes the names have not been checked for availability, and trade mark is the protection that does not depend on how the art was made.

## Checks of Astra 6 claims
- [unverifiable] The All Day page visibly discloses AI involvement and credits its animation to Ink Explainer. -- My fetch of the watch page returned only footer text. It is plausible: for animated content YouTube shows any disclosure in the expanded description only. Note that such disclosure is voluntary for clearly unrealistic animation, so its presence says nothing about monetization status.
- [partly] Narration, illustration, and editing tools can follow the roles shown in the tutorial (section 7). -- The tools themselves are permitted. But the tutorial's combination (transcript-derived script, stock ElevenLabs voice, AI stills assembled in CapCut) matches YouTube's named non-monetizable patterns. Astra's caveat about independent research is right but the report never states the platform risk.
- [partly] Voice stage: approved narration using an original or appropriately licensed voice. -- Licensing is necessary (ElevenLabs free tier is non-commercial) but not sufficient. The report misses that own-voice cloning is explicitly disclosure-exempt on YouTube and that a stock voice adds review and audience risk.
- [confirmed] Originality test: if both channels covered the same subject, would question, argument, scenes, jokes, thumbnail and ending still be recognizably different? -- This lines up with YouTube's 'interchangeable' wording and with the idea/expression rule. It should be extended to channel look (banner, colours, avatar), which the impersonation policy covers, and to variation between your own episodes.
- [confirmed] Changing a shirt color or replacing words in an existing script would leave most of the creative work borrowed. -- Consistent with the reused-content policy (no substantive modification) and with derivative-work risk in copyright.
- [partly] A recurring character (Mara, Nib or Mox) is the foundation of the show's own identity (sections 5-6). -- Sound creatively, and YouTube explicitly accepts AI used to visualize a unique character you invented. The report omits that a prompt-generated character is not copyrightable in the US and likely not in the EU; human-drawn design plus a trade mark is needed for it to be an ownable asset.
- [partly] Review stage: factual check, originality pass, mobile thumbnail check, complete playback (section 7). -- No policy gate. Missing: inauthentic-content self-check across the channel, disclosure decision, EU AI Act check for any realistic depiction, tax and VAT set-up, and keeping process evidence for appeals.
