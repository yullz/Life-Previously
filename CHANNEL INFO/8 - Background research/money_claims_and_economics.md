# Research: money_claims_and_economics

Collected 5 Oct 2026 by an independent research agent. Confidence labels are the agent's own.

## Headline
The "$98k/month" figure is not in the video. It is the caption a repost account (@HurricaneCrypt0) put on a Leo Grundström coaching-funnel video originally titled "$45,000/month", and the highest claim made in the video itself is $18,000 from one video. Ink Explainer plausibly earned $20-100K in its single viral month (mid case about $50-60K) but only about $3-14K in a normal month, with one video carrying 59.5% of all views. For a new solo channel the realistic expectation is $0 until Partner Program entry (the watch-hour bar doubles for applicants from 1 Feb 2027), then roughly $60 per 10,000 views. Time is the real cost, not tools, and the tutorial's clone pipeline matches what YouTube is demonetising.

## Implications
1. Treat the launch as a capped experiment, not an income plan: about 10-12 episodes, roughly 300 hours and $160-520 of tools over four months, with ad income assumed to be $0 during the test. Set stop/continue criteria in advance; do not forecast from Ink Explainer's one 10M-view video.
2. Change Astra's 6-9 minute target to about 8:30-12:00. Anything under 8:00 forfeits mid-roll ads, and Ink Explainer's own catalogue (14 of 17 videos at 8:00+, the hit at 11:13) is built on that line.
3. Niche RPM does not separate concepts A, B and C: history (about $9-10 estimated) and psychology (about $13 estimated) differ far less than channels within one niche ($2.31-$19.50). Choose the concept on hit potential of the titles and on hours per episode; B's reusable office set is the cheapest to produce, A's cutaway scenes the most expensive.
4. Timing matters if the user launches: reaching 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 watch hours before 1 Feb 2027 (about 17 weeks away) halves the watch-hour bar, roughly 53K versus 107K views. Do not trade originality for speed, and confirm in YouTube's own guide whether an application or an approval must precede the date.
5. Do not copy the tutorial pipeline (competitor transcript in, similar script out, stock ElevenLabs voice, one AI image per timestamp). Original research, a claim sheet with sources, a distinctive character, AI disclosure and ideally the user's own recorded voice are the monetisation insurance against an inauthentic-content ruling, not just an ethical choice.
6. Buy tools month-to-month on the lowest tiers (about $40-60 a month) and only after a small visual and voice test. The tool list in the source video carries affiliate links, so it is a sales recommendation, not evidence that this stack is needed.
7. Plan the first real income as sponsorship plus fan funding rather than AdSense: once videos reliably pass 10-20K views, one integration at $20-35 CPM can exceed the ad revenue of that video. Publish a business contact and simple media kit early; enable memberships at the 500-subscriber tier.
8. Do not pay for the coaching programme. The presenter's own teaching channel shows 2K-15K views per video and headline income figures that vary from $11.5K to $64K a month across his uploads, and his masterclass page carries a results-not-typical disclaimer.

## Gaps
- I read the caption track and post metadata only; I did not view video frames, so the on-screen revenue dashboards and whose channels they show are unverified.
- Ink Explainer's real RPM, audience geography, monetisation start date, sponsorship status and daily view curve are unknown; the best-30-day figure (8-10M views) is my estimate from totals.
- Higgsfield's official pricing page would not render; third-party sources conflict ($9-$79 tiers, 120-1,200 credits) and credits per image are unknown. Claude and CapCut prices are from memory.

- No cohort data found on how new faceless or AI-assisted explainer channels started in 2025-2026 actually perform (survival, time to Partner Program); base rates given are for channels in general.
- vidIQ's sample excludes zero-subscriber and unindexed channels, so 40.6% at 1K+ overstates the true share; the roughly 5% Partner Program share divides two differently defined numbers.
- AIR's RPM sample is 300 established channels (10K-50M subscribers) working with that network; a new small channel may sit well below the median.
- Sponsorship and membership figures come from SEO-style blogs aggregating surveys; no audited source was found.
- The Hollywood Reporter article was paywalled; I relied on The Next Web's summary of it.
- Whether applying before 1 Feb 2027 (versus being approved) is enough to use the old 4,000-hour bar was not confirmed.
- Hours per episode (20-40) is my own estimate, not a measurement; the user should time a pilot.
- The coaching programme's price (one third-party listing says $5,000) and the presenter's claims (700 students, $3M revenue) are unverified.
