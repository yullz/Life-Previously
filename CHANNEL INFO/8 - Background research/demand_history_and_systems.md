# Research: demand_history_and_systems

Collected 5 Oct 2026 by an independent research agent. Confidence labels are the agent's own.

## Headline
Demand evidence does not support Astra's preferred concept C: its three pilots (clock, receipts, queue) have no 2024-2026 outlier that I could find, and in three within-channel comparisons single-object or single-institution "origin" titles earned about 1K-12K while personal-stakes everyday-life titles on the same channels earned 100K-1M+. Concept A's logistics wording is also weak (direct siege-feeding and news-speed comparables: under 1K to about 57K), although visceral one-person logistics questions do produce outliers (SandRhoman 7.76M). The best-proven pool is ordinary people's everyday life in a specific era asked as the viewer's own question; it transfers beyond prehistory (Rome: 1.74M in 12 days on a 13.6K-sub channel) but is cloned within days and decays fast, so original packaging and less-mined eras matter.

## Implications
1. Do not make concept C ('We Made This Weird') the lead brand promise as defined. Its three pilots have the weakest comparables of the six, and single-object or single-institution origin titles earned about 1K-12K in every within-channel test found. If the user wants to keep it, run it as an occasional episode type with a second-person hook ('your surname', 'your workday') or as an 'every object in your kitchen/pocket' compilation, not as one object per episode.
2. Concept A is usable only if each title is a visceral question about one person's situation. Drop 'How Ice Became a Business' and 'Who Fed a City Under Siege?' as worded. Ice should be reframed around danger or summer survival, knowing Veritasium published the definitive ice-trade video in February 2026. Siege-food videos flopped even with personal wording on tiny channels, so treat that topic as unproven. 'How News Travelled' has no strong comparable and should be cut or folded into a day-in-the-life episode.
3. The best-supported promise sits between A and C: ordinary people's everyday life in a specific time and place, asked as the viewer's own question (day, night, sleep, heat and cold, food, toilet, boredom, work). It still covers both of the user's interests, because work, sleep, boredom and privacy are behavior topics set in history. Astra's characters (Mara or Mox) could be kept as a guide who visits a different era each episode.
4. Stay out of the prehistoric 'ancient humans' stick-figure lane and never reuse a hit title verbatim: identical-title copies got under 11K, and the originators themselves are down to roughly 3K-60K per upload. Prefer eras less mined in this format (Victorian or Edwardian city life, 1900s-1970s, Soviet daily life, Edo Japan, medieval town trades), accepting that demand there is less proven (Theoretico Soviet week 549K; Walter Reconstructs History 32K-328K for 20th-century America).
5. Change Astra's concept test so it measures topic demand and not only character appeal: add an era-everyday-life title next to the A and C titles in the 45-second-opener and thumbnail comparison, and choose the two full pilots from the candidate list (suggested first picks: night and sleep, work and jobs, heat and cold, hygiene, food and inns) instead of receipts, queue or clock.
6. Plan for a lottery-shaped outcome. Small channels in this space typically get about 2K-30K per video with rare 100K-1M+ outliers that fade within weeks. Budget for 10-15 videos before judging the channel, and do not project from Ink Explainer's single 10.5M video.
7. On length, Astra's provisional 6-9 minutes is at the short end of what is winning: Ink Explainer's top three run 11:13, 11:37 and 8:06, the Zenn and Axen hits about 8:30, and the Rome and Egypt channels 12-36 minutes. Planning 8-12 minutes is better supported.
8. Originality is a competitive requirement here, not only an ethical one. The space is flooded with look-alike uploads at 0-500 views, impersonator channel names exist (several 'Zenn' variants), and some successful channels now put 'NO AI' in their titles (This House, Chat History). Nothing in this data favors a particular tool; a distinct drawn character, visible sourcing and non-cloned titles are what separate the survivors.

## Checks of Astra 6 claims
- [wrong] Recommendation: develop the bridge concept C (rules, objects and systems humans invented) first. -- Not supported by demand evidence. No 2024-2026 outlier found for clock, receipts or queue; object/institution 'invention' titles earned 1K-12K on Deep Epoch, about 5.7K on Weirdward Bound, and 45K (guns) on Ink Explainer itself against a 293K median. Astra did say it was not market-validated.
- [confirmed] Familiar human concerns (cold, bored, hungry, tired, short of time) provide the entry point; historical explanation supplies the surprise. -- Matches every within-channel comparison I ran: personal survival and daily-life titles beat society-level or object titles by roughly 10-100x on Deep Epoch, Before Civilization and Weirdward Bound.
- [partly] All Day (10.4M) paired with 'NO JOBS' is the strongest example of the channel's packaging and shows broad relevance of work and leisure. -- It is the top video (10.55M on 5 Oct), but Astra missed that the angle was already proven: Axen's '...all Day Before Jobs Existed?' (2026-05-04, 3.37M), Zenn (2026-05-09, 440K) and Sleepy Time Historian (2025-12-31, 2.70M) came first. Ink Explainer followed an existing pattern.
- [partly] Concept A pilots: 'How Ice Became a Business Before Electricity', 'Who Fed a City Under Siege?', 'How News Travelled Faster Than Its Messenger'. -- The subjects have some audience (ice 11.87M on Veritasium with a 'deadly' hook; military siege videos 1.9M-2.8M), but as worded they match low comparables: direct siege-feeding videos 24-756 views, news-speed 55K-57K on established medieval channels.
- [wrong] Concept C pilots: 'How the Clock Became Everyone's Boss', 'Why We Invented Receipts', 'How a Queue Becomes a Society'. -- As demand bets these are the weakest of the six. Receipts: best comparable 25.8K (5 years old). Queue: nothing recent above 53K outside Disney-specific content. Clock: largest comparables are from 2023 or far older; recent attempts 23 views to 12K.
- [confirmed] IKEA Effect at about 16K: one older psychology upload is insufficient evidence to reject behavior as a niche. -- Zenn, a stick-figure channel in the same format family, has 'The Calhoun Effect' 4.9M, 'Why You Can't Remember Being a Baby' 1.8M and 'The Slotow Effect' 329K, alongside flops like 'The Nocebo Effect' 13K. - [confirmed] Terrifying Animal at about 216K: dramatic wording alone does not reproduce the biggest hit. -- 217K on 5 Oct. Consistent with wider data: even exact copies of a hit title on other channels got under 11K.
- [partly] Channel showed about 112,000 subscribers and 16 videos; top upload about 60% of views, top three about 76%, median about 313,000. -- Slightly stale. On 5 Oct the channel page shows 17 videos; my fetch of the videos tab gives a median of 293K and All Day at 10.55M. The concentration conclusion still holds.
- [unverifiable] Concept C thumbnails should show one familiar object behaving unexpectedly, with little text. -- I did not analyze thumbnails. Topic-level evidence suggests object-led packaging correlates with low views unless a viewer-stakes hook is present, so this needs a real test rather than assumption.

## Gaps
- Method: all numbers were fetched on 5 Oct 2026 directly from youtube.com search results, channel video tabs and watch pages (logged out, US/English). I did not use third-party trackers. Ages are YouTube relative labels except where an exact date is given.
- YouTube search is not exhaustive, so 'no outlier found' for clock, receipts and queue does not prove none exists. Results may vary by region and session.
- Public view counts only: no click-through, retention, revenue or audience data for any channel, so I cannot say why a video won.
- Survivorship bias: I found winners through search and popular tabs. The many 0-500-view uploads are visible but uncounted, so true hit rates in the everyday-life pool are unknown and probably low.
- The Deep Epoch comparison covers its latest 15 'Invent' titles plus two from its popular tab; the channel's overall decline is a confound. Subscriber counts at the time each hit was published are unknown.
- I did not watch the videos, verify their factual quality, or confirm which channels are AI-produced or share an operator (Rome Unscrolled lists Before Civilization as collaborator).
- Demand for modern-era everyday life (1900s, Soviet, mid-century) was sampled only lightly; evidence there is thinner than for ancient, Roman and medieval settings.
- Non-English markets were not checked, apart from one French counter-example (Dinguerie) that surfaced by accident.
- Monetization risk for mass-produced look-alike formats under YouTube's 2025 'inauthentic content' policy wording is from memory (medium confidence) and was not fetched or verified here.
