# Research: names_and_distinctiveness

Collected 5 Oct 2026 by an independent research agent. Confidence labels are the agent's own.

## Headline
None of Astra's three names passes a basic check cleanly: @TheSupplyRoom is taken and the phrase is generic, "Tiny Decisions" belongs to a popular spin-wheel app whose owner filed a US trademark in July 2026, and "We Made This Weird" is one word away from Pete Holmes's long-running "We Made It Weird". The character designs are safely far from Ink Explainer's look, but the raccoon and the envelope sit in crowded mascot territory, and I found no real evidence that a non-human mascot helps a channel about human life. Eight alternative names passed quick handle, channel, podcast, US-trademark and .com checks; "Who Agreed To This?" and "Life, Previously" are the strongest.

### F9 [medium] There is no solid public evidence that mascot-led explainer channels outperform mascot-less ones on YouTube; adjacent research shows only modest benefits for recognition and learning.

## Implications
1. Do not launch under any of Astra's three names as written. Drop "The Supply Room" (handle gone, generic) and "We Made This Weird" (confusable with a bigger show). "The Tiny Decisions Office" is the only one unique as a full string, but it would live in the shadow of the app and its pending trademark.
2. Shortlist "Who Agreed To This?" for the bridge or behavior direction and "Life, Previously" for the history-led direction. Claim the YouTube handle and the .com the same day you decide: look-alike channels in this name space are appearing roughly monthly.
3. Treat the name as hygiene, not a growth lever. Ink Explainer reached 113K subscribers with a generic name and a numbered handle (@Inkexplainer96), so effort belongs in titles and thumbnails.
4. Prefer a human lead, or at least a human cast acting out the scenes, over a raccoon or envelope protagonist. If a mascot is kept, use it as narrator or corner mark. In Astra's 45-second concept test, run the same opening with a human lead and with the mascot lead and compare.
5. If concept C goes ahead, redesign away from raccoon-with-bag (crowded archetype, six "Raccoon Explains" channels) and give it a palette that differs from concept B's; plum/mint/coral and violet/coral/turquoise are nearly the same.
6. Concept A's quartermaster is the most visually distinctive and the only human lead, so it carries the least mascot risk even though its name is the worst; the character can be kept under a different show name.
7. Concept C's topic lane (who invented the weekend, why tipping exists) already hosts channels with single-digit views, so a mascot plus that topic list is not a differentiator; the pilot must win on packaging and a viewer-in-the-scene hook.
8. Keep thumbnails away from Ink Explainer's grammar of a big stick-figure face plus a two- or three-word block caption. Astra's "one object, little text" direction is distinct but untested for click-through.
9. Before spending on logo or merchandise, run a proper clearance on the final name: USPTO search in class 41, EUIPO and UKIPO, plus X, Instagram and TikTok handles. My screen was US-only through a third-party index.

## Gaps
- A handle reported as "not found" means no channel currently answers at that handle (checked 5 Oct 2026 through YouTube's public resolve endpoint). YouTube may still reserve or refuse it; only the handle-claim screen confirms.
- Trademark screening was US-only through TrademarkElite. USPTO's own search, Justia and uspto.report returned 403; EUIPO, UKIPO and WIPO were not checked. This is not legal clearance and common-law rights were not assessed.
- X, Instagram and TikTok handles, Spotify's podcast directory and Google Play were not checked. The Google Play install figure for the Tiny Decisions app came only from a search snippet.
- I viewed three Ink Explainer thumbnails and two neighbour thumbnails, no video. Whether Ink Explainer uses a recurring named character or a corner mark in the player is unverified.
- No artwork exists for Mara, Nib or Mox, so visual-collision judgments rest on Astra's text descriptions. Comparisons to Tom Nook, Sly Cooper, Rocket Raccoon, object shows and paper-cutout channels are from memory.
- System1's often-quoted 41% versus 29.7% market-share figures appeared only in a search snippet (the LBB article returned 403), so I did not rely on them. The 180K YouTube subscriber figure for Pete Holmes's show is also snippet-only.
- No study was found on adult viewers' identification with animal versus human hosts in explainer video; the only direct study is on children aged 4-6.
- RSS view counts for micro channels can lag the live count, and small channels fail for many reasons, so the near-zero-view neighbours are anecdote, not proof about mascots or topics.
