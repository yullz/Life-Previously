## Six replacements for the remaining slots

### 5. **5 “Modern” Ideas That Are Actually Ancient**

**Format:** a countdown, moving from familiar comforts to the most surprising mechanism.

The Visitor tries to assemble a recognisable modern day from ancient ideas: buying prepared hot food, living in an apartment building, enjoying heated floors, paying a coin-operated dispenser, and walking through automatically opening doors. Each reveal answers three questions: how did it work, who could use it, and what was the catch? Distinguish surviving buildings from mechanisms described in ancient texts.

**Thumbnail:** the Visitor holding a coin beside a clearly reconstructed ancient dispenser. **“ANCIENT VENDING?”** One surprising object sells the episode; a collage of five objects would weaken it.

**Why it earns a slot:** it tests the broader list format you suggested, with specific discoveries instead of a sweeping claim that ancient people did everything better. The final mechanism gives the countdown a payoff. Each stop gets its own place and period label.

### 6. **Could You Get Through Medieval London Without a Fine?**

**Format:** a viewer-choice challenge.

Give the Visitor a simple evening errand. At each decision—going out after curfew, carrying something that counts as a weapon, choosing a lodging—let the viewer pick before revealing the actual rule and its exceptions. A small on-screen tally keeps the challenge moving. Anchor the episode in the London ordinances conventionally dated to 1364, rather than mixing centuries of unusual laws.

**Thumbnail:** the Visitor stopped by a night watchman, with a lantern between them. **“WHAT DID I DO?”**

**Why it earns a slot:** viewers participate. The humour comes from recognisable assumptions meeting unfamiliar rules. This gives us a different story from Episode 001’s day in London.

### 7. **Rich vs Poor: Dinner Before the French Revolution**

**Format:** two parallel stories, one meal.

Follow a working household and a wealthy household in Paris: obtaining ingredients, preparing dinner, serving it, and dealing with leftovers. Cut between the same steps so each contrast has a clear meaning. Investigate time, kitchen labour and affordability as well as what is on the plate. Lock the exact pre-revolutionary year and supported menus during research.

**Thumbnail:** two plates across one divided table, the Visitor between them. **“SAME CITY. DIFFERENT DINNER.”**

**Why it earns a slot:** food and inequality are instantly understandable. The visual contrast attracts attention; the people buying and cooking the food sustain the episode. Avoid an invented royal feast versus an equally invented starving family.

### 8. **Your First Day at Gladiator School: What Happens?**

**Format:** a first-day experience, with expectations tested against evidence.

The Visitor expects an arena entrance. Instead, explore accommodation, food, equipment, drills, supervision and the limits on a trainee’s freedom. Use Carnuntum’s archaeological complex as the physical anchor, with the year fixed during research. Clearly identify reconstructed routines and evidence borrowed from elsewhere; we do not have a complete surviving first-day timetable.

**Thumbnail:** the Visitor confronting a large training post while holding a wooden practice weapon. **“DAY ONE.”**

**Why it earns a slot:** gladiators are immediately recognisable, and the training premise gives us a focused human story. The appeal comes from the experience and its constraints, without needing graphic violence or invented fights.

### 9. **You Join the Gold Rush in 1849. Can You Get Rich?**

**Format:** a money-and-decisions challenge.

Start with a clearly hypothetical newcomer and historically sourced expenses. Follow the choices between buying equipment, paying for food and shelter, looking for gold, and finding paid work around other prospectors. Keep the running budget visibly illustrative; surviving records supply prices and individual experiences, not a guaranteed average fortune or failure date. Use San Francisco as the starting point and label any move to a mining district.

**Thumbnail:** the Visitor’s pan holds a tiny fleck of gold; a large bill sits beside it. **“RICH YET?”**

**Why it earns a slot:** it combines the fantasy of sudden wealth with the question viewers care about: what would actually happen to me? The ending judges the Visitor’s decisions using the evidence, rather than repeating the easy claim that every shovel seller won and every miner lost.

### 10. **Salem, 1692: Could You Prove You Weren’t a Witch?**

**Format:** an accusation story built around documents.

Follow an accusation into examination and the search for witnesses or support. At crucial points, let the viewer consider a response, then show how comparable documented cases unfolded. Use a clearly identified reconstruction or a named case; never manufacture testimony. The central tension is the difficulty of defending yourself when accusations and evidence work differently from what you expect.

**Thumbnail:** the Visitor facing an accusing finger and an open court record. **“PROVE IT.”**

**Why it earns a slot:** an immediately understandable, high-stakes question. The payoff is understanding the process and the people trapped in it. Keep it humane: no supernatural claims, guaranteed escape strategy, or invented “witch tests.”

## Research foundations

These are sources for developing the concepts, not completed script fact-checks. The four approved ideas retain their research routes in the [original proposal](next_10_video_ideas_2026-10-09.md).

- **Ancient ideas:** Pompeii’s archaeological park documents [hot-food counters](https://pompeiisites.org/en/press-releases/from-the-12th-august-the-thermopolium-of-regio-v-is-opening-to-the-public/); Ostia’s park identifies a [multi-storey apartment building](https://ostiaantica.cultura.gov.it/en/educational-panels-and-maps/larea-a-servizio-del-fiume/insula-del-soffitto-dipinto/); Bath preserves [hypocaust heating](https://www.romanbaths.co.uk/walkthrough). Hero’s [Pneumatics, sections 21 and 37–38](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/77400/pg77400-images.html) describes the coin mechanism and automatic temple doors. A described mechanism is not proof of widespread installation.
- **Medieval challenge:** translated [London ordinances in Florilegium Urbanum](https://the-orb.arlima.net/encyclop/culture/towns/florilegium/government/gvjust09.html). Preserve exemptions and distinguish a prescribed penalty from evidence that it was imposed.
- **Dinner comparison:** Reynald Abad’s research on [Paris’s food supply under the Ancien Régime](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hal/journl/hal-03945241.html) establishes the research route; the abstract was inspected, not the full book. Specific household menus still need direct evidence.
- **Gladiator school:** [Carnuntum’s archaeological account](https://www.carnuntum.at/de/gladiatur) distinguishes surviving evidence and interpretations of the training complex. Modern reenactors’ routines are not primary evidence for ancient daily schedules.
- **Gold Rush:** the National Park Service documents [San Francisco’s 1849 mail queues and associated businesses](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/mail-and-the-national-historic-trails.htm); the Library of Congress holds [Kimball Webster’s account of his 1849–1854 experiences](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/calbk/160.pdf), a research lead for the fuller experience.
- **Salem:** the University of Virginia provides an [overview and documentary archive](https://salem.lib.virginia.edu/further.html), including [Mary Warren’s examination records](https://salem.lib.virginia.edu/n135.html).
