# Ten video ideas for Life, Previously

**Proposed after the Egyptian workers episode · 9 October 2026**

**Selection update:** the owner approved this list's #1 (1709), #2 (pirates, with a pirate hat for the Visitor), #7 (Persian ice), and #8 (New York). The remaining six were not selected. The [current slate of four approved ideas and six replacements](next_10_video_ideas_selected_2026-10-09.md) supersedes this proposal list; its research notes remain available.

My recommendation is to develop **the winter of 1709 next**, followed by **pirate life between raids**. The Persian ice episode is my strongest visual curiosity idea. These picks combine a question viewers can understand immediately, a clear thumbnail, and everyday problems that can sustain a ten-minute story.

These are original working titles and editorial recommendations, not forecasts of views. The order below is a proposed sequence, not an approved publishing schedule.

## Research starting points and boundaries

The sources below establish promising research routes. They do **not** establish every proposed scene or clear a script for production. Each episode still needs its audience check, title/angle clearance, dated setting and fully checked claim ledger.

1. **Paris, 1709.** An analysis of Louis Morin’s observations provides evidence for the severe winter. Household coping and provisioning need additional contemporary sources. Avoid “coldest winter ever” and unsupported mortality figures. [Climate of the Past: Morin’s Paris observations](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/18/1685/2022/index.html).

2. **Queen Anne’s Revenge off the Carolina coast, 1718 — proposed anchor.** Wreck evidence offers a concrete route into food and shipboard life. Distinguish the vessel’s earlier slave-trading service from its pirate period; do not transfer another captain’s rules or Royal Navy routines to this crew. [North Carolina’s Queen Anne’s Revenge Project: food remains](https://www.qaronline.org/blog/2017-08-01/artifact-month-bone).

3. **Edo, around 1800 — year to lock during research.** The National Diet Library describes relaxing, talking and playing shogi after public bathing. Bath layout, fees and rules must be matched to our chosen period. [National Diet Library: Yukata, Edo Chic](https://www.ndl.go.jp/en/imagebank/column/yukata).

4. **London, 1851 — working frame.** Mayhew’s interviews offer individual occupations and circumstances. Choose one supported household; separate a trader’s sales, business costs and earnings, and avoid inventing an “average Victorian wage.” [London Museum: Henry Mayhew’s London](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/henry-mayhew-brings-victorian-london-life/).

5. **Hedeby, ninth century — exact house and year to select.** The museum reconstructs houses from archaeological finds. Use one dated building as our anchor; a floor plan alone cannot prove people’s feelings about privacy or universal sleeping arrangements. [Viking Museum Haithabu: exhibition and reconstructed houses](https://haithabu.de/de/dauerausstellung).

6. **Tlatelolco, 1519 — working frame.** UNAM’s market study identifies goods and source accounts. Check translations, dates and authors’ perspectives before quoting prices, currencies or crowd sizes. [UNAM: the market of Tlatelolco and its products](https://www.noticonquista.unam.mx/amoxtli/1890/1890).

7. **Yazd province, nineteenth century — town and year to verify.** The evidence describes winter ice stored for summer distribution. The ice-house itself was not a refrigerator for storing food. Do not turn this into an unsupported “ancient machine making ice in midday desert heat” claim. [Encyclopaedia Iranica: Yaḵčāl](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/yakcal/).

8. **New York’s Lower East Side, 1900.** The museum discusses a rent survey from that year and the limits of inferring individual rents. Keep later 1907–08 statistics separate and select a documented household or clearly identified composite. [Tenement Museum: the history of rent](https://www.tenement.org/blog/the-rent-is-due-a-history-of-rent-at-97-orchard-street/).

9. **Vindolanda, around AD 100 — year to lock against the tablets.** Museum material covers cooking equipment and barrack-room hearths. Separate ordinary soldiers’ food from commanders’ household purchases. Do not show Hadrian’s Wall in an AD 100 scene. [Vindolanda: Roman food and cooking](https://www.vindolanda.com/blog/food-and-drink-part-4).

10. **London, 1665 — working frame.** Archive documents offer a route into household restrictions and practical responses. Some documents in the collection are from 1666: obtain the relevant 1665 orders and evidence of practice before fixing scenes. [The National Archives: Great Plague sources](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/great-plague/).

## Why this mix

The slate alternates survival, humour, comfort, money, food and housing across different places and periods. Viewers get a fresh question each time, while the channel keeps its identity: ordinary people solving ordinary problems under very different conditions. Once our first releases have meaningful audience data, use their click-through and retention patterns to refine the order and packaging.
