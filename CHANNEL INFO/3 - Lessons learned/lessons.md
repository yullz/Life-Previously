## Size the Visitor from the picture, not from the default — 9 October 2026 (owner caught it, LP-L028)

- **The default pose size (0.60 of frame height) is not a size; it is a starting guess.** In episode 003 it made the Visitor a giant in an overhead street (standing on a wall, five times the residents, S086), turned a chapel into a model (S102), and made him 1.2-1.5x too tall in four more scenes. The owner flagged S086 and picked the smaller S102. Check every posed composite against what the background shows: next to a local at about the same distance he is about the same height; he is shorter than any doorway at his depth; nearer means larger; his feet are on open ground, never on a wall or roof. An eye-level figure cannot stand in an overhead view: use an eye-level background or no pose. Changes are logged in the episode's `build/visitor_placement_changes.json` for the director.

## One camera, one ground, believable sizes — 9 October 2026 (owner caught it, LP-L026)

- **An edit can splice two camera heights into one picture, and I accepted one.** Episode 003 S022 (man carrying a water jar) was an edit of the overhead village view: the left half came back at eye level, the right half kept the village seen from high above, so the lane looked like a ridge above the rooftops; the far-left house had a door half the man's height under an oversized window. I passed it on people count, props and style, and the owner caught it ("this picture doesn't look right"). When an eye-level shot is wanted, edit an accepted eye-level image of the same place, not an overhead base. A fix was ordered (repair from the eye-level street view S004); the check is unproven until the remaining images pass it.
- **Reserved space comes back blank, and "no furniture" deletes built-in furniture.** Asking the model to keep space for a card or the Visitor produced pale panels (S006, S064, S070, S071) and a pure-white strip across a quarter of S023. ## Sound
- **Level every effect against the voice, never with one flat volume.** Files differ by about 30 dB. `lp.py render` normalises each effect by its `kind` in `channel/sfx/sfx.json` (spot −26, ambience −30, accent −23 LUFS). *(Pilot: market sizzle, crowds and bells were louder than the narrator; the quill and splash were inaudible.)*
- **A sound must be what the picture shows.** A melodic chime reads as a phone notification; a bell must be a bell. Check by analysis (onsets, decay, inharmonic partials) before using any new effect. *(Pilot: marimba sting at 1:00.)*
- **No sound that makes no sense in the moment.** Ambience only where the scene has that source in view.
- **Ambience belongs to its era and surface.** The medieval street (cart wheels on mud, pigs) is wrong under Roman stone paving; an indoor tavern murmur is wrong on an outdoor street. Make era-specific ambiences (`crowd-roman-street`, `crowd-arena`) instead of reusing. *(Episode 002.)*
- **When a cue is flagged loud, compare it with the voice as the render processes it.** The render runs narration.wav through highpass + loudnorm and then the master gain, so the raw file reads about 6 dB too low. Process the narration the same way (see `voice =` in lp.py render), add the master gain, and measure the same window. In episode 002 I wrongly blamed S076's flag on the crowd by comparing the raw file; halving the crowd changed nothing, and the processed voice alone measured -7.8 against the mix's -7.9: it was the narrator's emphasis, as the first check had found. Test by measurement before changing a mix. *(Episode 002, S076.)*
- **Foreign words must be spoken right, and checked after recording.** Run `python pipeline/voice_diff.py <slug>` after every voice take: it re-transcribes the narration with a larger model and lists every place the audio differs from the script (most are digits or US spellings; read the rest). Respelling the coin "as" as "ahs" still came out as "hours", so the script says "copper" instead. Prefer an English rendering over a respelling when a word would be misheard; never add a bare common word like "as" to `voice.pronunciations`, because replacements are whole-word across every episode. *(Episode 002.)*
