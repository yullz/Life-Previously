## 1. What this is
**Life, Previously** (YouTube: **@LifePreviously**) is a faceless, illustrated history channel about how ordinary people actually lived, one named place and year per episode ("London, 1390", "Pompeii, AD 79"). Each long video runs 10-12 minutes (the owner's minimum is 10), plus three Shorts cut from it.

The look: flat 2D cartoon stills in one fixed style. One recurring character, **the Visitor** (a modern young man in an orange hoodie who stands in for the viewer), is composited from a fixed library of approved pose drawings. One ElevenLabs narrator voice. Real sources on screen. The goal is a show viewers take for a small studio's hand-made work, with **no visible AI tells**.

Everything is produced from this folder: research and fact-check, script, narration, images, assembly (`pipeline/lp.py`), checks, thumbnails, packaging, Shorts. The AI does the production work and hands it to the owner for review. After the owner's review and an explicit go-ahead, the AI can also set up and schedule the upload in YouTube Studio through the owner's browser (it did so for episode 001). Channel verification, legal and tax settings (such as country of residence) are the owner's.

## 3. Hard rules (never break these)
1. Never use another channel's video, transcript, script, title or thumbnail as input or model. Research comes from books, papers, primary-source editions and museum or university pages.
2. Every factual sentence in a script must trace to a row in that episode's `claims.md`. Add the claim with its source first, then write the line. Every claim must be fact-checked (source passage read, spoken wording confirmed, a separate critical attempt by Astra to refute it, honestly recorded as self-review) and marked `checked YYYY-MM-DD` before any voice, image or render work. `lp.py` blocks voice, image jobs and the final render until then.
3. Never let the image model draw the Visitor in a background. Use the `[pose: ...]` tag and describe only the background. A new pose goes into `channel/poses.json` and the pose library.
4. Narration must pass `python pipeline/lp.py check <slug>` with no banned-phrase warnings and must sound like natural speech. Add any AI-sounding phrase you notice to `banned_phrases` in `pipeline/config.json`.
5. Before anything that spends credits or money (images, poses, voice, music), state the count and estimated cost and wait for the owner's OK. When you submit paid jobs, save the returned job IDs immediately, and never resubmit a request whose outcome is unknown: check it first. A submission that failed outright (for example a 503 error) created no job and is safe to retry.
6. API keys live only in `.env` (`ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`). Never print, copy, upload or commit them.
7. Don't change the style, palette, character identity, image model or narrator in the middle of a season. Owner-authorized exception (9 Oct 2026): the Visitor may wear researched traditional/historical clothing appropriate to an episode, preserving his face, hair, proportions and identity. Clothing must be understated and fit the actual setting, local cast, weather and task; avoid conspicuous costume details. Use episode-private pose derivatives; normal clothing remains where no traditional outfit is selected. Read VIDEO DEVELOPMENT/WARDROBE_POLICY.md. Don't hand-renumber scene IDs (S001...); new scenes get new numbers.
8. Never permanently delete project files or images. Back up an image before changing it (episodes keep backups in `build/prefix_backup/`).

## 6. How an episode is made
Commands run from the folder root. `python pipeline/lp.py -h` lists the lp.py commands. The helper scripts in `pipeline/` (`compose_check.py`, `final_check.py`, `review_manifest.py`, `voice_diff.py`, `shorts.py`, `ocr_scan.ps1`) mostly take the episode slug (`compose_check.py` also takes a sheet name, e.g. `all`; `ocr_scan.ps1` takes a folder such as `episodes/<slug>/images`). Their usage is in the first lines of each file; don't pass `-h` to them.

1. **Topic:** check demand and that no other channel owns the angle (`channel/topics.md`). Create the folder: `python pipeline/lp.py new <NNN-slug> "<working title>"`.
2. **Research and fact-check** (`/episode-research` in Claude Code, or follow `.claude/commands/episode-research.md`): every claim goes into `claims.md` with its source; each one is checked and marked `checked YYYY-MM-DD`. Fill in `period.txt` (how people, buildings and light look in that place and year, and what the image model gets wrong), at the latest before the script's image prompts (`/episode-script` does it if research didn't).
3. **Script** (`/episode-script`): `script.txt`, 10-12 minutes (1,650-1,900 words at the measured 160 wpm; `lp.py check` warns outside 10-12.5 minutes), one image per line. Format is in `README.md` ("Script format"). Then a line-by-line fact pass. Run `python pipeline/lp.py check <slug>` until it says OK.
4. **Voice** (paid; ask first): `python pipeline/lp.py voice <slug>`, then `python pipeline/voice_diff.py <slug>` to catch misread words, and `--redo N` for weak chapters.
5. **Images** (paid; ask first; `/episode-images`): `lp.py jobs <slug>` writes the exact image requests to `build/jobs/*.json`. Audit every prompt against `channel/lessons.md` first. Buy a small test batch, inspect it, then buy the rest. Images are generated on Higgsfield (Nano Banana Pro, 2K, 16:9) with the channel's style key as reference. `lp.py fetch <jobname>` stages the results in `episodes/<slug>/build/candidates/<jobname>/` without touching `images/`; after the full-size inspection, `lp.py accept <jobname> [--only ID ...]` installs them and keeps every replaced file in `build/prefix_backup/`. Edits always start from the exact accepted local file, identified by episode, scene and content hash: if it was corrected locally, `lp.py jobs` stops and asks you to upload that file and record it with `lp.py refs --file <path> <media_id>`. Keep recurring props (fountains, water towers, wall notices) to roughly one street shot in five. `lp.py check` warns when fountains or water towers pass their share of all image prompts (`recurring_props` in config.json); check notices and other props by eye. Never use the retired poses `closeup-phone` and `phone-up` (`lp.py check` flags them); `drink-cup` and `parcel-overhead` hold pilot props, so check what a pose holds before using it. **Every image gets an independent full-size inspection before it is accepted.** Shots of the same place must match (compare them side by side).
6. **Composites:** `python pipeline/compose_check.py <slug> all` shows every Visitor placement: feet on the ground, right size next to the locals, not covering what the line is about.
7. **Render:** `python pipeline/lp.py render <slug> --preview --music music/life_previously_theme.mp3`, then the same without `--preview`. Output: `episodes/<slug>/output/<slug>.mp4` (the only deliverable). A final render refuses to start while any background, pose, sound effect, card overlay or the narration is missing or a placeholder, and it will not overwrite an existing master: render repairs as candidates with `--out episodes/<slug>/build/director_review/<name>.mp4` (captions `.srt` and a `.manifest.json` with every input and output hash are written beside it), and use `--replace-master` only after approval. Captions come from `pipeline/captions.py` (readable phrases of at most two 42-character lines, no flashes or overlaps); `python pipeline/captions.py <file.srt>` audits any caption file. Evidence the narration quotes (inscriptions, price lists) goes on screen as a designed card: `[card: name x,y width +seconds]` with the card in `episodes/<slug>/overlays/<name>.png`, never as lettering painted by the image model.
8. **Final checks:** `python pipeline/final_check.py <slug>` (`--video <candidate.mp4> --out <folder>` for a candidate; it fails on anything it cannot measure; loudness: -14 LUFS; the render aims at -2 dBTP and the check must show a true peak at or below -1 dBTP; no effect louder than the voice; if a cue is flagged loud, measure it against the voice as the render processes it, see lessons.md) and a frame-by-frame inspection of the finished video (`python pipeline/review_manifest.py <slug> [--video ... --out ...]` gives two frames per scene with its line and records the video's hash; two frames per scene are a sample, not a playback review).
9. **Package** (`/episode-package`): three titles, three thumbnails (`lp.py thumb`, rules in `channel/thumbnails.md`), description with sources, tags, chapters, captions. Write it all into `package.md`.
10. **Shorts:** write `shorts.json` (see episode 002's) and run `python pipeline/shorts.py <slug> --preview`, then `python pipeline/shorts.py <slug>` (`--video <candidate.mp4> --out <folder>` for review candidates). Free; uses the finished video. Each Short is written twice: `<id>.mp4` under 9.5 MB for the browser upload tool and `<id>_master.mp4` at full quality.
11. Tick every gate in the episode's `qa.md`, update section 5 of this file, and hand over.

**If you don't have the Higgsfield connector:** `python pipeline/lp.py brief <slug>` writes a helper page with every prompt for a manual run on the Higgsfield website (same model, Nano Banana Pro 2K, with the style key as reference), and `python pipeline/lp.py import <slug> <folder>` files the downloaded images under their scene IDs (see `lp.py import -h`). The job files in `build/jobs/*.json` also hold the exact requests. ## 8. The mistakes that cost the most (full list in `channel/lessons.md`)
- Unchecked facts were voiced and illustrated, then had to be redone. Now facts come first and `lp.py` blocks production until every claim is checked.
- Images were accepted from small contact sheets; about 80 faults (paper posters, medieval and modern objects, readable fake letters, impossible buildings) surfaced only after rendering. Now every image gets an independent full-size inspection *before* acceptance.
- The image model drifts toward its style reference (medieval London) and toward modern clichés. Name the period's own forms positively in `period.txt` and the prompts, and keep a precise avoid list.
- Never ask the model for letters or words; it paints gibberish. Real inscriptions go on screen as `[note: ...]` captions.
- To remove something from an image, ask the edit to *replace* it with the right thing; plain removal leaves stubs.
- A "same place, different moment" shot (empty, riot, night) should be an edit of the first image, not a new generation, or the place changes.
- Check the Visitor's scale and footing in every composite, and the props in his hands (old poses carry old props).
- Sound must fit the era and the picture, and never be louder than the voice. Measure; don't guess.
