# Research and boundaries

A fictional single adult's first-night search in 1900 contrasts nightly lodging, boarding and monthly family housing. Mills House No.1 was operating, and Riis's1900 edition—not only his 1902 rewrite—confirms20c lodging, 15c dinner and shared amenities. The9am–5pm room closure occurs in the 1902 book but was not located in the 1900 passage, so omit it. The 1900 census supplies actual male and female boarders at 97 Orchard, avoiding reliance on a 1908 citywide percentage. Veiller rent ranges belong to a different surveyed block; caption and speech say so.97 Orchard's apartments are small three-room homes, not three-bedroom homes. The Levines have three children in 1900, not the eventual five; workplace staff are not counted as residents. Do not use the Ellis Island main hall without choosing a date after itsDecember 1900 opening, or assume all arrivals passed through it. Avoid 1901 tenement reforms, 1903 women'shotel, 1904 subway, 1909 Mills/bridge imagery. Riis is a reform advocate with stigmatizing prejudices; factual observations can be used without adopting his moral explanations of poverty. No authenticity claim for fictional Visitor's bed or particular family's private agreement. Re-check 2026-10-10: the rent chapter speaks the 1900 Commission's block counts and one witness's rents (SRC08) instead of the museum article's floor ranges, which could not be re-read; the apartment figures follow the National Register form (SRC09) and no whole-building count is spoken because readable sources disagree (20 or 22).

Read: 2026-10-09 (Astra); 2026-10-10 re-read: Gutenberg text, Mills Houses passage ('a regular dinner of good quality is served at fifteen cents. The night's lodging is twenty cents'; baths, free library, paid and self-service laundry; 'bedrooms are necessarily small, but they are clean'; No. 1 in Bleecker Street with room for 1554 guests, both houses filled and crowds often turned away; intended for 'decent mechanics, laborers, and men of small means').

Use/limits: Reform advocate's account, not neutral inspection of all lodging. Use concrete prices/facilities, not stigmatizing judgments or comparisons asserting identical Waldorf beds.

## SRC08 — DeForest and Veiller (eds) — The Tenement House Problem, including the Report of the New York State Tenement House Commission of 1900, vol. 1 (Macmillan, 1903)

https://archive.org/stream/tenementhousepro01deforich/tenementhousepro01deforich_djvu.txt

Read: 2026-10-10: title page and contents; pp.112–113 (Veiller, 'Tenement House Reform in New York City, 1834–1900': the exhibition block bounded by Chrystie, Forsyth, Canal and Bayard streets, 1 January 1900: 39 tenements, 605 apartments, 2,781 persons, 263 two-room, 179 three-room, 105 four-room and 21 five-room apartments, 264 water-closets, not one bath, rentals $113,964 a year); p.400 ('Tenement Evils as Seen by the Tenants': 'Several took boarders. Use/limits: A reform commission's publication: the block was chosen as 'characteristic', not measured as a citywide average, and the rents are one witness's experience, not a tariff. Do not transfer the block's counts or the witness's rents to 97 Orchard Street.

## SRC09 — National Park Service — National Register of Historic Places registration form, Tenement Building at 97 Orchard Street (NRIS 92000556, 1992)

https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/92000556_text

Read: 2026-10-10: section 7 description: erected 1863–4; stoop replaced and toilets and shaft built in 1905; 'The second through fifth floors each contain a central hall with wooden floor, two toilet compartments, and four apartments'; 'Each apartment consists of three small rooms a living room or parlor, central kitchen, and inner bedroom'; parlor about 11 by 12 feet 6 inches (about 138 sq ft), inner bedroom about 8 feet 6 inches square (about 67 sq ft); 'Each apartment totaled about 325 square feet'; south-side partitions moved in 1905.

Use/limits: A 1992 measurement of the surviving building. Sources differ on the whole-building apartment count (20 or 22), so none is spoken. Hall toilets date from 1905, so none in 1900. No modern floor plan is drawn.
