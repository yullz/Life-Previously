# Research and boundaries

A fictional single adult's first-night search in1900 contrasts nightly lodging, boarding and monthly family housing. Mills House No.1 was operating, and Riis's1900edition—not only his1902rewrite—confirms20c lodging,15c dinner and shared amenities. The9am–5pm room closure occurs in the1902book but was not located in the1900passage, so omit it. The1900census supplies actual male and female boarders at97Orchard, avoiding reliance on a1908citywide percentage. Veiller rent ranges belong to a different surveyed block; caption and speech say so.97Orchard's apartments are small three-room homes, not three-bedroom homes. The Levines have three children in1900, not the eventual five; workplace staff are not counted as residents. Do not use the Ellis Island main hall without choosing a date after itsDecember1900opening, or assume all arrivals passed through it. Avoid1901tenement reforms,1903women'shotel,1904subway,1909Mills/bridge imagery. Riis is a reform advocate with stigmatizing prejudices; factual observations can be used without adopting his moral explanations of poverty. No authenticity claim for fictional Visitor's bed or particular family's private agreement.
