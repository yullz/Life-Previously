# Research and boundaries

# Research direction

The episode is one imagined warm-season night in Kaifeng around 1120. Its spine is food → demanding restaurant service → wine-house spectacle → tea and entertainment → the workers still awake → dawn. The primary memoir supplies concrete details and also its own warning: the author recalls a city he lost, admits hearsay and omissions, and is not surveying every household. Use short attributed explanations rather than claiming universal prosperity.

Read the specified Chinese sections directly. Independent challenge: later Hangzhou material is explicitly excluded; the third and fifth watches are not exact modern clock times; Sichuan restaurants do not imply chili peppers; a daytime scroll is not proof of night lighting. The title's 900 years is deliberately approximate.

The Visitor's clothing is a restrained plain jacket and trousers derived from the inspected Northern Song everyday-clothing detail. No rank insignia, dragon silk, elaborate headwear or altered face. Keep his clothes less visually dominant than the food and street action.


## SRC01 — Meng Yuanlao, Dongjing meng Hua lu, preface

https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/東京夢華

Read: Complete preface read, including nostalgic framing, departure after warfare, hearsay/omission acknowledgement and Shaoxing dingmao date.

Use/limits: Retrospective memoir with declared selectivity, not a census or proof that everyone prospered.

## SRC02 — Dongjing meng Hua lu, volume 2: night markets, streets and wine houses

https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/東京夢華錄/卷二

Read: Zhou Bridge night market; East Corner Tower streets; Panlou eastern streets; wine-house opening paragraphs read in Chinese.

Use/limits: Seasonal lists kept separate; third-watch wording not converted into a precise universal closing time. Grand wine-house description not every shop.

## SRC03 — Dongjing meng Hua lu, volume 3: late markets, fire prevention and dawn

https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/東京夢華錄/卷三

Read: Maxing street shops, night/early reopening, late tea sellers, fire-prevention and dawn-market sections read.

Use/limits: Busy districts sometimes continuous; quiet streets differ. No precise modern clock equivalence or imaginary fire event.

## SRC04 — Dongjing meng Hua lu, volume 4: restaurants and food trades

https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/東京夢華錄/卷四

Read: Restaurant ordering/service, regional specialities, mistakes and penalties, cake/bread shops and fish sections read.

Use/limits: Do not illustrate twenty bowls balanced on an arm as a reliably measured everyday norm; service anecdote attributed. No exact prices used.

## SRC05 — Dongjing meng Hua lu, volume 5: performers in the capital

https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/東京夢華錄/卷五

Read: Complete Jingwa jiyi list through daily performances and music rehearsal paragraph.

Use/limits: Lists performers/genres, not a timetable for our fictional evening. No imported later Beijing opera costume.

## SRC06 — Dorothee Schaab-Hanke, The Capital Behind the Capital (2011)

https://www.schaab-hanke.de/publikationen/Schaab-Hanke_OE_50_2011_The_Capital_Behind_the_Capital.pdf

Read: PDF p4 (printed193), pp9–10 (198–199) relevant restaurant comparison and table p17 (206); text distinguishes early-12th-century Kaifeng from later Hangzhou.

Use/limits: Only read passages relied upon. Later Ducheng jisheng anecdotes not imported into Kaifeng. Translation of obscure food names not treated as a modern recipe.

## SRC07 — Palace Museum: Zhang Zeduan, Along the River During the Qingming Festival

https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/paint/228226.html

Read: Object description and discussion of shops, varied social statuses and clothing read.

Use/limits: Song artwork is selected/composed representation, not a photographic street survey. Original Northern Song work, not a later Qing remake.
