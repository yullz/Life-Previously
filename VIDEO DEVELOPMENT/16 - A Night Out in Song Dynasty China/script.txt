TITLE: What Was a Night Out Like in China 900 Years Ago?

It is dark, you are hungry, and the street ahead is still selling dinner. Welcome to a night out in China, nine centuries ago.

::: S003 | Simple original China location map with Kaifeng dot, no modern national boundary claim or decorative pagodas. [still]

::: S005 | Four simple stops as narrative order only: market, restaurant, entertainment, dawn. No false walking-distance map. [still]

::: S008 | Three separate simple dishes: flatbread, plain noodles and mutton bowl. Original illustration, no exact recipe claim. [pan-right]
Farther into his descriptions come flatbreads, noodles, different eating houses and regional specialities. Dinner is not one identical bowl for everybody.

And the evening has time to develop. Meng says night markets can continue well into the night, with the busiest places continuing until dawn.

::: S010 | Same district with one darker side lane and a late pedestrian, food light visible behind; preserve street geometry. [pose: glance-nervous 0.8,0.94 0.6] [pan-left]
That does not mean every street is a party. His account also distinguishes quieter places, and people coming home late from their business.

::: S011 | Customer considers stall and nearby restaurant doorway, clear distance and modest buildings; no modern menu board. [same-as: S001] [zoom-out]
For us, the next decision is pleasantly simple. Eat standing near a stall, or go inside and let somebody take our order?

## A city with more than one dinner
::: S013 | Two tables receive visibly different simple bowls; one noodles, one meat dish, no identical cloned meals. [same-as: S012] [zoom-in]
Inside, the meal need not be whatever one large pot happens to contain. Meng describes restaurants offering different foods and preparations.

::: S014 | Two locally typeset restaurant categories beside neutral bowls, no modern provincial borders or chilli icons. [still]
He mentions Sichuan-style restaurants and establishments serving southern food. People could encounter regional differences without leaving the capital for another province.

::: S015 | Plain noodle and fish dishes beside chopsticks; no takeout carton, fortune cookie, chilli or plastic tray. [same-as: S008] [zoom-out]
Those names are not permission to put a modern takeaway menu on the table. We are following the dishes and choices in this source.

::: S016 | Customer at table watches server approach, another table eating; differing choices visible without textual menu. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S012] [pan-right]
The important point is the range. A city meal could involve choosing an establishment, then choosing what you wanted it to prepare.

::: S017 | Summer bowl and winter hearth icons separated with explicit season labels; not one simultaneous stall inventory. [still]
At the street stalls, the season changes the offer too. Meng gives separate summer and winter lists near Zhou Bridge.

::: S018 | Small ceramic drinking bowl near a modest chilled vessel, restrained indication of cold; no modern ice cubes or fridge. [note: Summer offerings · Meng, volume 2] [same-as: S008] [pan-left]
His summer list even includes iced cooling drinks, among them liquorice water. A warm evening could have its own cold temptation.

::: S019 | Drink displayed simply with source-caption space; no modern ingredients list, price or health claim. [same-as: S008] [zoom-out]
We are not reconstructing the exact recipe, or promising every passer-by could buy one. It is a named offering in his account.

::: S020 | Anonymous writer's hand beside blank page; small original food vignettes added locally around page, not actual handwriting. [same-as: S004] [pan-right]
That variety is part of the pleasure of reading him. He remembers particular foods, particular places, and the choices between them.

::: S021 | Customers and workers vary in restrained clothing and activity; no automatic identical wealth or extravagant feast. [same-as: S001] [zoom-in]

::: S022 | Server beside one table holding small slip/paper and chopsticks only if visually workable; no readable writing or modern pad. [same-as: S012] [pan-left]
For tonight's imagined visit, we have chosen a modest meal. The server arrives, and our simple order joins everybody else's particular instructions.

::: S023 | Two diners gesture modestly toward distinct bowl positions; server attentive, no speech text painted. [pose: closeup-worried 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S012] [zoom-out]
Somebody wants one preparation. Another wants something different. The challenge is not merely carrying a bowl. It is remembering whose bowl is whose.

::: S024 | Server faces serving hatch, cook behind it listens; same hatch orientation as dining-room anchor. [note: Meng, volume 4 · restaurants] [pan-right]
Meng takes us close enough to hear the process: orders gathered at the tables, then called toward the kitchen for preparation.

## Your order is somebody else's test
::: S025 | Server beside table, three distinct small bowl icons as local overlay for different requests, no literal dialogue. [same-as: S012] [zoom-in]
The server asks each diner what they want. Hot, cold, or prepared another way: Meng describes customers making their own particular requests.

::: S026 | Server speaks toward cook across hatch; no shouting caricature, cooking fire safely behind cook. [same-as: S024] [pan-left]
Those choices have to reach the kitchen. The server calls them out, so the people preparing the food know what has been asked for.

::: S027 | Server carries three manageable bowls toward diners, clear route between tables. No impossible twenty-bowl tower. [pose: watch-arms-crossed 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S012] [zoom-out]
Then the dishes must return to the right customers. Several tables can become several small tests of memory, balance and attention.

::: S028 | Three bowls securely supported with plausible hand positions; no extra fingers, stacking on head or shoulders. [same-as: S008] [pan-right]
Meng admires astonishing feats of bowl carrying. We do not need to stage an impossible balancing act to understand why the skill mattered.

::: S029 | Illustrative diner pauses over bowl while server checks table; no claim this actual incident happened. [same-as: S012] [zoom-in]
Imagine waiting for a hot dish and receiving the wrong order. For the customer, a disappointment. For the server, potentially something worse.

::: S030 | Brief attributed source card, neutral list of possible consequences without coins or invented wage amount. [still]
The writer says a complaint could bring a reprimand, a loss of wages, or even dismissal. Correct service came with pressure behind it.

::: S031 | Server carefully sets correct bowl before diner; kitchen staff visible beyond hatch, calm serious focus. [same-as: S012] [zoom-out]
That is his account, rather than a rule we can impose on every employer. But it changes how we watch the room.

::: S032 | Diners settled and eating while server moves through clear aisle; Visitor listens at edge. [pose: closeup-listening 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S012] [pan-right]
Our evening feels leisurely because somebody else is concentrating. A complicated order gives the customer choice and gives the worker more to remember.

::: S033 | Single noodle bowl set on table beside correctly placed chopsticks; no hand clipping through ceramic. [same-as: S008] [zoom-in]
So when our bowl arrives, let us at least know what we asked for. The person carrying it has enough to keep track of.

::: S034 | Cook passes one bowl through established hatch while another awaits preparation, coherent progression of food. [same-as: S024] [pan-left]
The scene feels surprisingly recognisable: different tastes, a busy kitchen, and somebody trying to get dinner to the right person while it matters.

::: S035 | View from dining-room doorway toward distant warmly lit building, without changing interior layout. [same-as: S012] [zoom-out]
Still, dinner is only one part of the city after dark. Outside, some establishments are trying to impress you before you even enter.

## When the evening becomes tomorrow

::: S074 | Five abstract night segments labelled watch divisions, with no fabricated hour labels; explain approximate sequence only. [still]

::: S075 | Some shutters closed, one tea stall active, another vendor begins setting up; preserve established architecture. [same-as: S010] [zoom-out]
