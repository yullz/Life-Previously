# Research and boundaries

Walk the audience through a patent-based Memphis1917shop, with labelled1916launch-ad flashbacks. The main reveal is stated in Saunders's own purpose: the route brings the customer past the assortment while reducing order-fetching labour. The cashier remains; this is not self-checkout. Read original6-page patent, including3drawing sheets; visual geometry checked in browser. First advertisement humorously discourages sightseers; second names ready-packed goods,5lbsugar in a cloth bag, baskets/cashier and no delivery. Keep intended business benefits distinct from proven cost or sales results. September6versus11opening discrepancy is unnecessary to the story; use month/year. Do not adopt museum children's summary's universal first-supermarket claim, one-product-old-shop caricature, or claim that choice alone caused lower prices. Smithsonian article loosely mentions carts in early model; original specifies baskets and1937object confirms the model's modern-cart exclusion. LOCphoto is c1918in or near Memphis, not securely first-store1916; catalogue read but image not inspected, so optional only. No competitor-channel material was used.

## SRC01 — Clarence Saunders, US1,242,872, Self-serving store (1917)

https://patents.google.com/patent/US1242872A/en

Read: Complete description and claims read; all three original drawing sheets visually inspected through Google Patents full-size images in browser on2026-10-09. Fig2 route, Fig1 lobby perspective and Fig3–4 galleries/cabinet section compared with description.

Use/limits: A patent describes claimed design and inventor's intended benefits, not independent proof of sales multiples, profit-margin reductions or identical layout in every store. Do not use OCR's garbled personal name/patent number. Use US1,242,872.

## SRC02 — Memphis Commercial Appeal launch advertisements, September3,1916, reproduced by Tennessee History for Kids

https://www.tnhistoryforkids.org/history/people/clarence-saunders/

Read: Both original advertisements visually read in full at linked images1916_09_03_MCA_1.jpg and1916_09_03_MCA_2.jpg.
