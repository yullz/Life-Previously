# Research and boundaries

Use the familiar human-alarm premise to reveal a small service business and answer who woke the knocker-up. Main bookends are Waugh's Manchester1862 observation. Most of the episode is explicitly a retrospective account published in1877 from an unnamed northern town,not a Manchester biography. Entire primary article read,including the easily omitted sentence crediting both husband AND alarm. Alarm clocks already existed; the title refers to household reliance,not invention. Modern research's Leeds assignment and1847 priority wording are not adopted. Fees remain weekly personal rates; income is not presented as typical,author's competition warning retained. Waugh's cotton-famine context blocks a generic booming1862city scene. Initial exact-script and two final slate reviews remain pending.

## SRC01 — Edwin Waugh, Home-Life of the Lancashire Factory Folk during the Cotton Famine (1862), chapter IX

https://www.gutenberg.org/files/10126/10126-h/10126-h.htm

Read: 2026-10-09: preface, opening context and complete chapter IX opening Manchester-to-Preston passage read.

Use/limits: One morning observation of a knocker-up going home to bed between six and eight; not every worker's schedule. Cotton-famine context must remain visible.

## SRC02 — Experiences of a Knocker-Up, Chambers's Journal no.725,17November1877,pp721–724

https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/50841/pg50841-images.html

Read: 2026-10-09: complete article from opening interview through author's final note read in sequence.

Use/limits: Anonymous mediated, literary interview. Mrs Waters' reported recollections, not independently audited career data. Town only B—; never silently assign her to Manchester or Leeds. Distinguish customer-specific rates from a national tariff.
