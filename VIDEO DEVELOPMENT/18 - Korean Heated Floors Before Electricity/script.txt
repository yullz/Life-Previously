## Follow the smoke
::: S013 | Canonical labelled side section; kitchen right lower, room left upper. [note: SIMPLIFIED SECTION] [still]
In this cutaway, fire burns in a low hearth beside the room. The occupied floor sits above its route.

::: S014 | Same section stage two, orange gas arrows below slabs only. [still]
Hot gases travel from the firebox into passages beneath the floor. Those passages carry heat along the underside of the stones.

::: S015 | Exploded detail stone, earth, paper with correct order and no air gap into room. [still]
The stones warm up. Earth and a paper surface complete the layers in the arrangement shown here.

::: S016 | Close section reassembled; solid floor closure legible. [still]
The paper is not stretched across an open fire. Solid layers separate the surface from the channel below.

::: S017 | Back outside chimney outlet; sparse smoke outside, no smoke through room door. [same-as: S001] [zoom-in]
Meanwhile, the smoke continues towards an outside chimney. The goal is to warm the room without sending that smoke through it.

::: S018 | Two separate arrow systems: orange gases below, amber heat through slab, labelled locally. [still]
That distinction is essential. Warmth comes through the floor; the combustion gases have a separate route out of the building.

::: S019 | Same slab after reduced flame; residual warmth subtle amber, no timer. [still]
The stone floor holds some of that heat. It can remain warm after the fire has settled, rather than cooling immediately.

::: S020 | Evening room in natural colours, no glow or thermometer. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S002] [pan-right]
How long depends on the building, the firing and the conditions. This house has no magical warmth guarantee.

::: S021 | Two restrained plan sketches marked examples; no performance ranking. [still]

::: S022 | Return to single canonical side section; other examples gone. [note: ILLUSTRATIVE PLAN] [still]

## Dinner and heating share a fire

::: S026 | Local adult tending pot, other adult brings small fuel bundle; no modern cookware. [same-as: S012] [pan-left]

::: S027 | Hands place modest brushwood beside hearth safely, no huge logs. [pose: watch-arms-crossed 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S012] [zoom-out]

## Comfort has limits
::: S061 | Visitor considering room, no magic icon or false numerical claim. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S002] [zoom-in]
It is tempting to imagine a perfect room: warm forever, free to run and pleasant in every corner.

::: S062 | Hands adjusting fuel with plain tool; no dangerous use demonstration. [same-as: S012] [pan-left]
The evidence gives us something more interesting. This was a practical technology, built and tended by people, with real constraints.

::: S063 | Cold exterior and warm room visible through small doorway, no thermal-camera rendering. [same-as: S001] [zoom-out]
Fuel still had to arrive. Heat still escaped from the building. Stored warmth was useful precisely because the fire did not do everything instantly.

::: S064 | Separate illustrative temple cell, sparse floor and window, no host from main story. [note: SEPARATE EXAMPLE · BISHOP'S TEMPLE STAY] [pan-right]

::: S065 | Same separate cell, open window, no thermometer or measured heat gradient. [note: ONE TRAVELLER'S EXPERIENCE] [same-as: S064] [zoom-in]

## An old idea without a single fixed design
::: S073 | Two simple labelled silhouettes, partial and whole-floor, no exact date ticks. [still]
Another trap is treating every heated floor across Korean history as if it looked exactly like ours.

::: S074 | Partial strip compared with whole-room channels, explanatory only. [still]
Earlier arrangements could heat only part of a room. Whole-floor systems and their construction developed over time, with different layouts.

::: S075 | Main house exterior at dusk, no ancient inventor character. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S001] [zoom-out]
The history is more varied than one invention moment. We need no legendary first inventor to explain tonight's house.

::: S076 | Clearly labelled modern hydronic pipe schematic separate from historical section. [note: MODERN COMPARISON] [still]
Modern heated apartment floors use different machinery. They can circulate hot water through pipes beneath the room.

::: S077 | Side-by-side historic flue and modern pipe, no mixed arrows. [still]
The comfort connects them. A boiler and pipework are different from smoke passing beneath stone slabs.
