# Research and boundaries

The episode is a practical shopping puzzle at Fort Albany,1733. Start with twenty hooks versus a blanket, then reveal why 'one beaver' is a unit rather than a simple animal count. Anchor numeric statements to the original Dobbs1744scan; pp.193–194 were visually inspected on2026-10-09. Original lists blanket6,breeches3,8knives/1,20hooks/1,kettle1lb/1,sugar2lb/1,broadcloth1yard/2,thread1lb/2. Modern heritage-derived tables often flatten the right column and misstate blanket/breeches prices; the UVic compilation independently mistranscribes other rows. Reject both for numeric authority. Use two source-based surprises: a kettle's weight changes its price, and Indigenous customers actively assessed imported cloth. Context is specific and relationships unequal; neither naive-barter caricature nor universal golden-age claim. No actual1733shopping receipt, exact household budget, exact fort plan or finished costume reference is claimed. The final six-unit choice is our arithmetic demonstration. Primary text is colonial and argumentative, not neutral access to Indigenous voices; source labels remain clear. Source-image and garment shape gate is separate from these factual checks.

## SRC01 — Arthur Dobbs, An Account of the Countries adjoining to Hudson's Bay (1744), Standard of Trade, pp.193–197

https://archive.org/details/accountofcountri01dobb/page/193/mode/1up

Read: Original OCR pp.193–197 read; pp.193–194 inspected in the browser at readable enlargement on 2026-10-09. A second 1744 scan (cihm_35075) cross-checked. Printed 1733 date, blanket six, pound-based brass-kettle rate, breeches three, hooks twenty and knives eight confirmed visually.

Use/limits: A published trade schedule and colonial author's account, not a receipt for our invented customer. Dobbs argued against the Company's monopoly and promoted expansion. Do not adopt his moral judgments, geographical claims or 'fair price' assumptions. The OCR misreads 1733 as1753. Modern summaries and the UVic transcription contain material numeric errors.

## SRC02 — Archives of Manitoba, Hudson's Bay Company Archives: Glossaries

https://www.gov.mb.ca/chc/archives/hbca/glossaries.html

Read: Made beaver, factory, account books, standard of trade and credit terminology read.

Use/limits: Definitions cover a long period; not evidence that later tokens, ranks or boats existed here in1733.

## SRC03 — Canadian Museum of History, Sealed and Delivered (2017)

https://www.historymuseum.ca/blog/sealed-and-delivered

Read: Entire article read, including seal contexts, cloth-quality assessment and English/French competition.

Use/limits: Regional interpretation across multiple sites and centuries. Do not transplant the pictured French Mackinac seal into Fort Albany or claim its design was universal. Some-post cloth share is not a1733Albany statistic.

## SRC04 — Canadian Museum of History, Virtual Museum of New France: Fur Trade

https://www.historymuseum.ca/virtual-museum-of-new-france/economic-activities/fur-trade/

Read: European market and pelt-to-felt sections read; underfur, felt and coat/parchment distinctions.

Use/limits: Use only the broad material explanation; do not transplant French legal arrangements or exact French prices into the English post.

## SRC05 — Richard Preston, James Bay Cree: respect relations within the great community of persons

https://www.richardpreston.ca/Anthropology/james-bay-cree-document

Read: Eighteenth-century ethnohistorical section read completely, including Whatting1706, credit1726–1737 and the author's explicit interpretation/identity caveats.

Use/limits: Scholarly synthesis citing Williams and Bishop. Do not assert all Albany traders were Cree, place the1757debt dispute in1733, or present the author's proposed leadership interpretation as uncontested fact.

## SRC06 — Ann M. Carlos and Frank D. Lewis, Property Rights, Standards of Living, and Economic Growth: Western Canadian Cree (2010 working paper)

https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/67758/1/623444887.pdf

Read: PDFp6/printedp4 read fully: wooded region, birchbark canoe trade and seasonal activities. Bibliographic title/authors checked.

Use/limits: Regional synthesis, not a particular family's diary. Do not use its unreviewed living-standard model or call the era a universal golden age. Ignore the passage's directional shorthand 'up river' when mapping a trip to a coastal post.

Read: Tariff section used as a discovery aid, then checked against the original scan.
