# Research and boundaries

A personal challenge rather than another general day-in-London tour. Three escalating temptations—carry a sword, win an argument, keep walking after dusk—reveal how peacekeeping touched ordinary movement and relationships. Date scenario c.1364. Critical chronology finding: Alsford's metadata says c.1364, but Riley's translated prefatory writ reads12June37EdwardIII (1363); do not declare1364 the law's exact enactment. The story's chosen penalty scale is pp.335–336, where a non-bloody fist strike is3s. Riley p.408 prints2s in a different ordinance, so do not merge scales. Curfew exception requires reputation/service, good cause AND light, not a universal night ban or lantern permit. Read a real1365case as a separate evidence layer: combined charges and same-day release on mainprise, no fine stated. The scenario succeeds only inside the fictional challenge; no guarantee these choices eliminated every legal risk. No universalizing of elite rhetoric about inns or poverty. Avoid money conversion to modern dollars. Distinguish city fine from victim recovery. All narrative and image violence remains mild and non-graphic. Bell cue must be an actual low church bell, not the notification-like cue rejected in episode001.
