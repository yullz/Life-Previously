# Research and boundaries

A personal challenge rather than another general day-in-London tour. Three escalating temptations—carry a sword, win an argument, keep walking after dusk—reveal how peacekeeping touched ordinary movement and relationships. Date scenario c.1364. Critical chronology finding: Alsford's metadata says c.1364, but Riley's translated prefatory writ reads 12 June 37 EdwardIII (1363); do not declare 1364 the law's exact enactment. The story's chosen penalty scale is pp.335–336, where a non-bloody fist strike is3s. Riley p.408 prints2s in a different ordinance, so do not merge scales. Curfew exception requires reputation/service, good cause AND light, not a universal night ban or lantern permit. Read a real 1365 case as a separate evidence layer: combined charges and same-day release on mainprise, no fine stated. The scenario succeeds only inside the fictional challenge; no guarantee these choices eliminated every legal risk. No universalizing of elite rhetoric about inns or poverty. Avoid money conversion to modern dollars. Distinguish city fine from victim recovery. All narrative and image violence remains mild and non-graphic. Bell cue must be an actual low church bell, not the notification-like cue rejected in episode 001.

## SRC01 — Liber Albus, translated by H. T. Riley (1861), pp. 334–336

https://archive.org/details/liberalbuswhite00carpgoog

Read: 2026-10-09 (Astra); 2026-10-10 re-read on the archive.org OCR text, pp.334–336: the writ 'Witness myself, at Westminster, this twelfth day of June, in the year of our reign thirty-seven'; 'Of keeping the peace': 'that no one be so daring as to go wandering about within the said city, or in the suburbs, after the hour of curfew rung out at the church of Our Lady at Bow, unless he be a man known to be of good repute, or his servant, for some good cause, and that with a light; the which curfew shall be rung at the said church between the day and the night … taken and sent unto the prison of Newgate, there to remain until he shall have paid a fine unto the City for such contempt, and have found good surety for his good behaviour'; 'That no one go armed': 'no one, of whatever condition he be, go armed in the said city or in the suburbs, or carry arms, by day or by night, except the vadlets of the great lords of the land, carrying the swords of their masters in their presence, and the serjeants-at-arms of … the King … and the officers of the City, and such persons as shall come in their company in aid of them, at their command … under the penalty aforesaid, and the loss of their arms and armour'; 'Of Hostelers': 'every hosteler and herbergeour cause warning to be given unto his guests that they leave their arms in their hostels … through default of warning by his host, such host is to be punished by imprisonment and by fine, at the discretion of the Mayor and Aldermen'; arrest by 'every man of standing in the said city, Alderman and commoner, who is of good repute … in the absence of the officers … unto the houses of the Sheriffs'; 'That no one draw sword or knife': 'provided he do not strike, he is to pay unto the City half a mark, or remain in the prison of Newgate fifteen days. And if he draw blood of any one … twenty shillings, or remain in prison forty days … with the fist, but have not drawn blood … three shillings, or be imprisoned eight days … draw blood with the fist … forty pence, or be imprisoned twelve days. And let such offenders find good surety, before their release, for their good behaviour. And nevertheless, let him upon whom such offence has been committed, have his recovery by process of law; and let such offences, as to blood shed … be pleaded before the Sheriffs from day to day, without any essoin or other delay'; 'Of good Watches': 'each Alderman cause to be set in his Ward good and proper watches … if evil happen through default of the watches, the Alderman and all the commons of the Ward may be answerable … each Alderman have the names of all those who are inhabitants, and dwellers with the inhabitants, of his Ward; as well of those who are put in retired places to work as of others'.

Use/limits: The writ is dated 12 June, regnal year 37 of Edward III (1363), whereas later editorial dating is c.1364. Set fictional day c.1364 without claiming enactment then. Do not mix p.408's different two-shilling fist penalty with p.336's three shillings.

## SRC02 — Stephen Alsford, Florilegium Urbanum — Ordinances for preserving law and order

https://the-orb.arlima.net/encyclop/culture/towns/florilegium/government/gvjust09.html

Read: 2026-10-09 (Astra); 2026-10-10 re-read: same clauses in Alsford's translation ('a man of known and good reputation, or his servant, [going about] for a good cause, and with a light'; 'No-one is to go about armed'; host 'punished by imprisonment and a fine to be determined by the mayor and aldermen'; '6s.8d or being imprisoned at Newgate for 15 days'; '20s. to the city or be imprisoned for 40 days'; '3s. … or be imprisoned for 8 days'; '3s.4d … or be imprisoned for 12 days'; 'offenders are to find reliable guarantors for their good behaviour'; 'Each alderman is to have the names of all those who are inhabitants'); notes on the Bow bells and the citizen's arrest.

Use/limits: Independent translation check, not independent evidence of actual enforcement. Its c.1364 dating differs from the explicit regnal date in the writ. Do not adopt the commentary's generalized criminalizing descriptions of inn guests.

## SRC03 — A. H. Thomas, Calendar of Plea and Memoranda Rolls, volume 2, Roll A10 (1364–5)

https://www.british-history.ac.uk/plea-memoranda-rolls/vol2/pp1-28

Read: 2026-10-09 (Astra); 2026-10-10 re-read: under 6 March 1365: 'William Moot, brewer, John de Henham and Thomas Fraunceys, hakeneymen, were attached to answer charges of being concerned in an affray by night in Candlewickstreet Ward, of keeping their doors open after curfew and of being nightwalkers against the peace. They were committed to prison, but the same day were mainprised to keep the peace and released.' The entry follows the Curteys detinue case.

Use/limits: Multiple charges together: affray at night, doors open after curfew and nightwalking. Imprisoned and released same day on mainprise to keep peace. No fine amount specified; no claim the release erased charges or all defendants had this outcome.
