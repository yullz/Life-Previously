# Research and boundaries

Set Paris1783, using a composite modest household and a composite affluent urban household. Avoid substituting Versailles for every prosperous city house. Main twist: a cabbage can be an elaborate meat-garnish recipe, and access to fuel/cooks/shop ovens changes what reaches a plate. Mercier is a vivid contemporary critic, not a neutral dataset. His own ordinary-meal account (soup,boiled beef,Sunday mutton) refutes a blanket meatless-poor narrative. His sausage anecdote supports a small purchase, not an invented complete daily ration. Read his surrounding social polemic to avoid adopting stereotypes or fantastical statistical certainty. Menon's1755Paris cookbook independently supports exact preparation sequences and planned reuse of roast poultry, challenging the idea that all rich leftovers were waste. It is a prescription, so the narrator explicitly chooses a period recipe for the reconstruction. No unverified causal claim linking one menu directly to revolution. Food and labour remain the story; methodological qualifications are concise. No modern dollar conversions or precise household food budget. Bibliographic date verified by Wellcome. Cairn's2004butcher-hygiene study was inaccessible except snippets and is not used.

## SRC01 — Louis-Sébastien Mercier — Tableau de Paris, chapter67, Les Halles (1782)

https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Tableau_de_Paris/067

Read: 2026-10-09: full chapter, pp216–220, including market, ordinary meals, poor household sausage and servants.

Use/limits: Contemporary polemic, not a consumption survey. Attribute his observations; reject universal superlatives, exact population fraction, literal extravagant early-pea price and causal claim that rich demand alone explains all food prices.

## SRC02 — Mercier — chapter362, Pâtissiers, Rôtisseurs (1783)

https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Tableau_de_Paris/362

Read: 2026-10-09: full chapter on shop displays, household dishes sent to ovens, fuel saving and roast-food convenience.

Use/limits: Use practice, not claimed universal oven loads or every roast burnt. His quoted2solbaking/10solwood comparison is an observation, not standard1783price schedule; omit exact amounts.

## SRC03 — Mercier — chapter383, Cuisiniers (1783)

https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Tableau_de_Paris/383

Read: 2026-10-09: complete chapter on affluent kitchens, sauces, labour, serving and leftovers.

Use/limits: Strong satire, staged dialogue and hostile stereotypes. No turtle-price anecdote, universal wage ratio, quarter-eaten statistic or prejudiced portrayals treated as fact. Servant comparison explicitly attributed.

## SRC04 — Mercier — chapter631, École de Boulangerie (1783)

https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Tableau_de_Paris/631

Read: 2026-10-09: full chapter; passage calling bread a principal food of urban poor.

Use/limits: Use bounded bread centrality. Omit inaccurate population, all-plants-edible/utopian famine claims, nutrition advice and sweeping contempt for cooks or other nations.

## SRC05 — Menon — Les Soupers de la Cour (Paris,1755), volumeIII

https://archive.org/details/lessoupersdelaco03meno

Read: 2026-10-09: title/imprint, p24 cabbage recipe, pp26–28 rissoles and pp29–33 roast preparation/seasonal poultry.

Use/limits: Public-domain prescriptive cookbook, not a menu served by a named1783family. Recipe stages can be shown; do not offer outdated meat handling as modern advice.
