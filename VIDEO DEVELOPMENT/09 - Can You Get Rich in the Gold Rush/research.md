# Research and boundaries

Centre on Bidwell's Bar, Feather River, October–November1849. Webster's sequence gives a real decision arc: $6/day with board at diversion works, independent mining and shared outlay, promising five-day return, rain and dear provisions, barely covering expenses, then$4.50/day building work with board. This is a retrospective memoir with contemporary material, not an audited ledger or universal miner biography. Taylor independently illustrates difficult prospecting and uncertain camp gossip, not the same camp. NPS supplies a separate San Francisco alternative-income vignette. NMAI corrects the empty-land premise and witnesses' colonial biases. Exclude Webster's racist and pseudomedical assertions. Known group purchases total$62 plus unpriced items, not$62 per miner. Nearly one ounce valued at$16 gives nearly$16 gross during one reported good spell, never a regular net wage. Rainy-period net figure unavailable. Date/weekday mismatch in memoir means use lateOctober only. No later hydraulic monitors, foreign-miners tax, modern dam, inflation comparison or success probability.

## SRC01 — Kimball Webster — The Gold Seekers of '49, chapter V (published 1917)

https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/71572/pg71572-images.html

Read: 2026-10-09: chapter V pp100–118 read continuously through prospects, prices, Bidwell work, purchases, returns, rain, tent and Burch offer.

Use/limits: Retrospective edited memoir, not audited accounts. Attribute his prices and returns; no statewide average. Reject racism, moonlight blindness, salt-causes-scurvy, doctor fatalism and virtually-no-theft claims. October26 weekday is inconsistent; omit exact first-work date.
