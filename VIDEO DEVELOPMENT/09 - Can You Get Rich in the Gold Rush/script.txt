## A job with meals

At Bidwell's Bar, Webster and two companions agreed to work on a river-diversion dam for six dollars each per day, with board.

::: S014 | Temporary diversion channel skirting low barrier, no full-scale reservoir or exposed fantasy gold vein. [same-as: S012] [pan-left]

::: S015 | Two workers carry loaded wooden hand barrow with coherent grips and path. [pose: closeup-worried 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S012] [zoom-out]

::: S016 | Personal wage card and simple meal icon, no lodging icon. [still]

::: S019 | Two choices on parchment with no invented expected-value percentages. [still]

::: S020 | Visitor views river from safe bank as three men move toward equipment. [pose: shrug-smirk 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S004] [pan-right]

## Buying your independence
::: S025 | Same rocker on curved runners, aged wood intact; local cost overlay only. [note: GROUP PURCHASE: $50] [same-as: S024] [zoom-in]
They paid fifty dollars for an old cradle: a wooden machine used to wash gold-bearing dirt, also called a rocker.

::: S026 | Two plain pans, one kettle, pick and shovel clearly separate; local labels on safe margins. [same-as: S002] [pan-left]
Two tin pans cost eight dollars together. A kettle added another four. They also bought a pick and shovel, whose price is not given.

::: S027 | 50+8+4=62, conspicuous GROUP label and unknown-tools note. [still]
That is at least sixty-two dollars in the purchases we can price. It is the group's total, not sixty-two dollars charged to each man.

::: S028 | Group-cost card stays without personal-share arithmetic. [still]
Nor can we invent each person's bill. The account does not explain the full arrangement of shares.

::: S029 | One worker adds dirt to upper hopper, another holds water vessel, third grips rocker; all stationary. [same-as: S024] [zoom-in]
Now it must earn something. Dirt goes into the screen, water is added, and the cradle is rocked.

::: S030 | Local rocker sectional schematic, downward water/material arrows and riffles; no impossible uphill flow. [still]
Coarse material stays in the screen. Below it, washing removes lighter material while heavier gold remains.

::: S031 | Trio observes neighbouring rocker across small gap; similar machine with distinct people. [same-as: S024] [zoom-out]
Watching is not the same as learning. Webster's group copied experienced miners as best they could.

::: S032 | Full hopper of gravel, water poised, no animated overflow implied. [same-as: S024] [pan-right]
Initially, they pushed through as much dirt as possible. A busy day felt like a productive one.

::: S033 | Dark concentrate with tiny flakes, no clean pile yet. [pose: closeup-worried 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S001] [zoom-in]
Then came the awkward part. Their collected material still contained black sand mixed with gold.

::: S034 | Miner tilts pan gently; mixed material still visible, no complete success state. [same-as: S001] [pan-left]
They struggled to separate it, and could not tell how much their day's work had produced.

::: S035 | Trio studies pan at dusk, tools set aside, no ledger numbers. [pose: scratch-head 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S001] [zoom-out]
You can be exhausted without knowing whether the effort paid. The answer is still mixed up in the pan.

::: S036 | Opposite-bank working recess identified as different claim within same river setting, no unlabelled property takeover. [same-as: S004] [pan-right]
They moved to another claim. Several days later, finishing the separation revealed a result worth getting excited about.

## The number everybody wants to hear
::: S037 | Small separated gold dust sample beside remaining black sand, not a full ounce rendered to scale. [same-as: S001] [zoom-in]
Webster says that after five days on that claim, they had averaged nearly one ounce of gold each per day. An encouraging beginning.

::: S038 | Small balance scale and gold dust packet, blank weights, local source label. [note: WEBSTER'S REPORTED EXCHANGE VALUE] [same-as: S002] [pan-left]
He describes gold dust exchanging at sixteen dollars an ounce, which helps explain their excitement.

::: S039 | NEARLY1oz × reported$16/oz, label GROSS VALUE, no equals exact$16. [still]

::: S040 | Gold dust on scale left, provision bundle and kettle right, no automatic balanced value. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S002] [pan-right]

::: S041 | Two cards: wage+board versus reported gross find; no misleading shared axis. [still]

::: S042 | Trio carefully stores small gold sample in plain pouch, no despair over real success. [same-as: S001] [pan-left]

::: S043 | Working riverbank under clear sky, darker cloud edge far away, one sun only. [same-as: S004] [zoom-out]

::: S044 | Same trio rests beside rocker while cloud cover increases; do not raise water yet. [pose: glance-nervous 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S004] [pan-right]

::: S047 | Three bedrolls under oak, late-evening clear sky, no romantic luxury. [pose: closeup-listening 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S045] [zoom-out]

::: S048 | Edit dry river anchor to wet higher water, exact ridge and oaks retained, subdued grey daylight. [pan-right]

## When the river takes back the workplace
::: S049 | Former dry gravel platform now underwater; recognisable red bank and oak. [pose: look-around 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S048] [zoom-in]
Webster dates the change to late October. Rain fell repeatedly, and the rising river covered bars where miners had been working.

::: S050 | Rocker moved safely up bank, trio looks toward covered bar; no workers submerged. [same-as: S048] [pan-left]
Equipment cannot earn anything on a flooded working place. Getting gold out became much harder.

::: S051 | Loaded mule and handler stopped beside soft track, no animal drowning. [zoom-out]
The trouble also travelled along the supply route. Mud made it difficult to bring provisions in by the usual pack animals.

::: S052 | Two arrows: WORK INTERRUPTED and SUPPLIES DISRUPTED, no unsupported exact volumes. [still]
Two problems came together: less opportunity to earn, and more difficulty obtaining necessities.

::: S053 | Plain flour sack in damp store, locally composited before/after price card with clear attribution. [note: WEBSTER'S ACCOUNT • PER POUND] [same-as: S002] [zoom-in]
In Webster's account, flour rose from forty cents a pound to a dollar and fifty cents. His local example, not a universal price list.

::: S054 | Identical dollar symbol beside smaller flour icon after price rise; label illustrative, not actual ration. [still]
Their money now bought much less flour. Some gold might still leave little after paying for necessities.

::: S055 | Same camp under rain, rolled blankets gathered off ground, no unsafe fire beneath wet tree. [same-as: S045] [zoom-out]
Sleeping outside also became a practical problem as the ground grew wet.

::: S056 | Shared tent interior with multiple blankets and narrow aisle, principal trio near opening. [pose: closeup-listening 0.8,1.02 0.82] [pan-right]
They found space in a large tent belonging to other miners, and each paid two dollars a week for the privilege of sleeping there.

::: S057 | Personal blankets on ground beside tent pole, no mattress or room partition. [same-as: S056] [zoom-in]
They still supplied their own blankets. The payment bought shelter, not a private hotel room with a bed and meals included.

::: S058 | Separate meal icon for job, shelter icon for tent, no merged package. [still]
That is a different purchase from the board included in their earlier wage. Combining the two would produce a very misleading budget.

::: S059 | Trio sits around cold covered provision sack, ordinary expressions, no starvation caricature. [pose: closeup-worried 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S056] [zoom-out]
Imagine your new week: less work, dearer food, and a fresh charge to sleep under canvas.

::: S060 | Small gold sample in hand beside plain folded blanket, no disappearing coins. [same-as: S001] [pan-right]
Gold is still valuable. Getting it out and meeting your expenses have become the harder part.

## The rush needed more than miners

The offer was four dollars and fifty cents each per day, with board. Webster and his companion agreed to take the work.

::: S074 | Three accurately qualified cards, no false continuous wage graph. [still]

::: S075 | Timber workbench and simple food bundle, no claim of permanent security. [same-as: S008] [zoom-out]

::: S076 | Workers build while provisions are delivered by ordinary handler, no triumphant merchant caricature. [same-as: S008] [pan-right]

## Whose opportunity?
::: S085 | Same landscape with established paths and river access; no invented attack scene. [same-as: S084] [zoom-in]
California's Indigenous peoples had long histories here. The rush came on top of earlier colonization, bringing further dispossession, resource loss and violence.

::: S086 | Simple homeland/arrival map with broad California outline, no unsourced tribal boundaries. [still]
For those communities, newcomers' dreams of wealth could mean threats to land, livelihoods and safety. The story cannot begin with an empty map.

::: S087 | River, oak gathering landscape and maintained path, no claim all people vanished. [same-as: S084] [zoom-out]
Native communities survived and continued rebuilding their lives. Recognizing their experience changes what a celebration of the rush leaves out.

::: S088 | Two short questions, no numerical moral-balance graphic. [still]
Our Visitor's question is about getting rich. History also asks whose opportunities were supported, and whose losses made those opportunities possible.

::: S089 | Two men work calmly at house frame, wet valley beyond. [pose: closeup-listening 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S008] [zoom-in]
For Webster's small group, the answer in this stretch of eighteen forty-nine was mixed: real gold, difficult costs, and a change of occupation.

::: S090 | Return to original small gold sample and pan, identical scale and no magically increased amount. [same-as: S001] [pan-left]
Their story does not settle every miner's fate. It shows why one exciting daily return can mislead.
