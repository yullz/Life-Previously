## Cold enough for a palace
::: S013 | Blank observation sheets on plain desk; no invented handwriting or modern computer. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [note: Louis Morin • Paris1709] [same-as: S011] [zoom-in]
Louis Morin repeatedly recorded Paris's weather. Modern researchers have used those observations to understand how severe the winter became.

::: S014 | Two labelled boxes joined by an arrow: early readings and modern interpretation; no drawn thermometer. [still]
The measurement conventions were not ours. Making old readings comparable takes historical detective work, not just copying numbers.

::: S015 | Three categorical blocks: severe cold, thaw, renewed cold; not a quantitative chart. [note: Paris weather sequence • schematic] [still]
The record shows severe cold interrupted by milder spells, not one unchanging freeze from beginning to end.

::: S016 | Small elite room with lit fireplace and bottle on mantel; no people. [pose: look-around 0.8,0.94 0.6] [note: Versailles • January1709] [pan-right]
First, take a short trip out of Paris to Versailles, where you might expect resources to solve everything.

::: S017 | One bottle tilted above one glass at left, a few small ice fragments at mouth; two natural hands attached to one cropped period-clothed adult. [same-as: S016] [zoom-in]

::: S018 | Bottle and glass on mantel at left, small fire below; no local guests. [pose: closeup-worried 0.8,1.02 0.82] [note: Saint-Simon's account • Versailles1709] [same-as: S016] [still]
That is his experience, not proof that every French drink froze. Still, it makes an uncomfortable dinner image.

::: S019 | Same compact elite room, clear foreground right; bottle and fireplace unobscured. [pose: shrug-smirk 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S016] [zoom-out]

::: S020 | Return to the original modest room, short wood bundle left, small fire, no locals. [pose: watch-arms-crossed 0.8,0.94 0.6] [note: Paris • January1709] [same-as: S001] [pan-right]

::: S021 | Close view of wood bundle beside stone hearth, no human hands or modern stove. [same-as: S002] [zoom-in]

::: S022 | Fire low but still present at left, unoccupied right foreground at safe distance. [pose: eyebrow-raise 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S001] [still]

::: S023 | Two adults carry separate small bundles beside the same masonry street; ground remains clear at right. [same-as: S003] [zoom-out]

## When the road to dinner breaks
::: S025 | Winter quay viewed from bank; moored wooden cargo boat immobilised by ice; one distant adult on quay. [pose: watch-arms-crossed 0.8,0.94 0.6] [note: Accounts of the freeze •1709] [same-as: S010] [zoom-in]
Saint-Simon writes of the Seine freezing. Other accounts describe ice and difficult roads obstructing the movement of food and wood.

::: S026 | One small wooden handcart halted beside an icy patch at left, one adult beside it; no catastrophe. [same-as: S003] [pan-left]
Not every road closes for the entire winter. Disruption is enough when a household needs supplies every day.

::: S027 | Same hearth-side position with only one short log remaining; no people. [same-as: S002] [zoom-out]
Picture a delayed delivery. At home, the consequence is simply that the expected wood has not arrived.

::: S028 | Hearth and bowl at left, separately composited character area clear. Fire modest, not extinguished. [pose: scratch-head 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S001] [still]
People have not forgotten how to light fires. They are keeping familiar routines working under unfamiliar pressure.

::: S029 | Separate locally drawn log and coin icons, labelled availability and affordability; no period currency denominations. [still]
You could have money and still struggle to obtain a delivery. You could find something for sale and struggle to pay.

::: S030 | Empty wood space beside hearth occupies left focal area, clear right floor. [pose: empty-bowl 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S001] [pan-left]
Different problems. At home, they look remarkably similar: an empty space where something useful should be.

::: S031 | Bread table and covered grain sacks, one established seller at left, clear right foreground. [zoom-out]
Now replace firewood with grain. The same question stretches outward from a kitchen, through markets and transport, into the surrounding countryside.

::: S032 | One round brown loaf on board, its crumb visible at a cut edge; no people. [same-as: S006] [pan-right]
A loaf is the end of a chain of work. Your stomach only notices the last link.

::: S033 | Established seller and two round loaves at left table, no customer besides separately composited separately composited character. [pose: clutch-stomach 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S031] [still]
This is why an ordinary shopping trip can become the centre of a historical crisis. You are not shopping for comfort.

::: S034 | Flat dormant grain field with thin patchy snow and stone boundary wall, no people. [pan-left]
You are trying to keep the household going. And the weather is threatening something much larger than today's delivery.

::: S035 | Low view of sparse damaged grain shoots in cold soil, no bodies, people or ice crystals exaggerated. [same-as: S034] [zoom-out]
Beyond the city lie crops that are supposed to become future meals. Damage there can follow you home months later.

::: S036 | A locally drawn loaf links to three labelled seasonal boxes; no fabricated dates or data. [still]
The bread problem is about to acquire a calendar.

## The price of the next loaf

## The thaw is not a reset button
::: S049 | Same field refrozen after thaw, sparse exposed damaged shoots, no heavy fresh snow or people. [same-as: S034] [zoom-in]
Milder weather does not mean the danger is finished. Cold returns, and the alternation of thaw and frost is damaging to crops.

::: S050 | Repeat categorical cold/thaw/cold sequence with middle block highlighted; no exact plotted temperature. [note: Paris weather sequence • schematic] [still]
Remember those observations: separate cold spells, with a hopeful interval between two very bad ones.

::: S051 | Brown spring field with sparse dead winter shoots, no ripe crop, no people. [pose: look-around 0.8,0.94 0.6] [note: Spring1709 • illustrative field] [same-as: S034] [zoom-out]
Now imagine discovering that food expected from a field will not arrive as planned. Waiting for warmth cannot replace that harvest.

::: S052 | Small bowl of barley seed beside a plain seed sack at field edge; no ripe ears or people. [same-as: S051] [pan-right]
Someone must grow something else. An inconvenient delay when you are hungry now.

::: S053 | One farmer at left scatters seed close to ground from plain sack; spring soil, no snow. [pose: watch-arms-crossed 0.8,0.94 0.6] [note: Saint-Simon • replacement barley in1709] [same-as: S051] [zoom-in]
Saint-Simon describes farmers sowing barley where wheat had been lost. Replacement sowing became one of the ways people responded.

::: S054 | Blank administrative sheet beside a small locally illustrated barley seed motif; no fake seal or handwriting. [note: Barley resowing • April1709] [same-as: S011] [pan-left]
The historian's account of the government's response includes permission to resow barley in April. Decisions about fields mattered far beyond them.

::: S055 | Established farmer and seed sack at far left, clear right ground, no mature crop. [pose: eyebrow-raise 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S051] [still]
Barley is not a magical crop that cancels a disaster. It is a practical response when the previous plan has failed.

::: S056 | Farmer tends the same spring plot with a plain hoe at left; no instant transformation. [same-as: S051] [pan-right]
You hoped for a secret survival trick. History offers agricultural work, uncertain results, and another wait.

::: S057 | Two labelled simple icons, hearth and growing grain; time distinction, no numerical claim. [still]
It also exposes the distance between surviving tonight and surviving the year. A fire answers one question. A harvest answers another.

::: S058 | Early spring version of established street, no snow; two adults walk carrying empty bowls, clear right foreground. [same-as: S003] [pan-left]
Meanwhile, our Paris household searches for help as longer solutions remain seeds in the ground.

::: S059 | Original room in neutral spring light, one empty bowl on table, no winter window frost. [pose: empty-bowl 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S001] [still]
Your cupboard is not interested in promising developments. It would prefer a bowl of something that exists already.

::: S060 | One period server with ladle beside large pot at left; no queue yet, clear right ground. [pan-right]
Fortunately, growing more food is not the only response people make.

## The people keeping people alive
::: S061 | Server at left table, three adult recipients separated in a short queue at centre-left; clear right foreground. [pose: look-around 0.8,0.94 0.6] [note: Food relief • Paris1709 reconstruction] [same-as: S060] [zoom-in]
Relief includes soup distribution, charitable giving and public work. Survival is also a story about assistance, not only private endurance.

::: S062 | One filled plain bowl on table, ladle above cooking pot behind; no cropped unattached hands. [same-as: S060] [pan-left]
A bowl handed across a table changes the household's immediate problem. It does not need to solve the entire kingdom to matter.

::: S063 | Server and pot left, one waiting adult at centre-left; right foreground unoccupied. [pose: closeup-worried 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S060] [still]
This is not unlimited assistance, available whenever anyone needs it. Need and help are not identical amounts.

::: S064 | Closed blank memoir beside two simple locally composited household icons; no wealthy villain scene. [note: Saint-Simon • charity and hardship in1709] [same-as: S011] [pan-right]

::: S065 | Two plainly dressed adults speak near established doorway at left, serious but not hostile, spring street. [same-as: S003] [zoom-in]

::: S066 | Same modest relief table, bowl clearly visible at left, no celebratory expressions. [pose: watch-arms-crossed 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S060] [still]
This is not a contest where the most resourceful family automatically wins. Resources, connections and available help affect what is possible.

::: S067 | Two plainly dressed adults speak near established doorway at left; spring street, calm gestures; clear right foreground. [pose: watch-arms-crossed 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S003] [zoom-out]

::: S068 | Simple locally drawn household silhouettes at different ages; no statistical proportions or mortality chart. [note: La clef du cabinet · March 1709] [still]
One contemporary account singled out children and older people without adequate protection. Survival was not a fair test of individual effort.

::: S069 | Return to same server, pot and three adult recipients; reconstruction scene with no archival texture or fake scan border. [note: Illustrative reconstruction • Paris1709] [same-as: S060] [zoom-in]

## Summer, and still a bread problem

::: S074 | One adult works with spade at left; another waits beside small bread distribution table centre-left; no modern tools. [same-as: S072] [pan-left]

## What actually gets you through
