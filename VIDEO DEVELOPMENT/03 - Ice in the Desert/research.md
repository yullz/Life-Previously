# Research and boundaries

Reconstruct a seasonal cycle at Meybod around 1900. This date is an editorial anchor within the documented long use of the type, not a claim to a preserved daybook. The architecture and functions are drawn from field research and engineering papers. Key contradiction resolved: later papers loosely call these ancient refrigerators, while Jørgensen explicitly distinguishes ice storage from food refrigeration. Say ice cooled drinks and could be used elsewhere; do not put hanging food inside the store. Avoid 400 BCE origin claims, exact historical freezing yields, fixed melt percentages, huge radiant-cold arrows, mechanical windcatchers, and a claim that a small dome vent creates the freezing. Engineering models are explanatory, not measurements from 1900; their own reported yields vary with assumptions. Some METU wording says transfer on hot summer days in its wall section; do not use that to move winter harvesting into summer. Water, operating labor and maintenance are central to the story. Final costume follows late-Qajar ordinary layered clothing, not court finery.

## SRC01 — Hemming Jørgensen — Yaḵčāl, Encyclopaedia Iranica

https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/yakcal/

Read: 2026-10-09: full entry, typology, ice production, ownership and survey; author conducted 2007–2009 site survey.

Use/limits: Strong historical synthesis grounded in field research. Pits' ancient origins do not establish the date of Meybod's dome; the article warns chronology is uncertain. Ice stores are distinct from food refrigerators.

## SRC02 — Pochee, Gunstone and Wilton — Passive ice making and seasonal storage (2017)

https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10087896/

Read: 2026-10-09: complete eight-page paper, printed pp.3579–3586; model assumptions and limitations included.

Use/limits: Modern simulations using a typical meteorological year, not weather observations from 1900 or measured historical output. Do not use model percentages as historical facts.

## SRC03 — Hosseini and Namazian — An Overview of Iranian Ice Repositories (2012)

https://open.metu.edu.tr/handle/11511/50831

Read: 2026-10-09: pp.223–231 read, including architectural elements, cited travelers, access and drainage. PDF at https://open.metu.edu.tr/bitstream/handle/11511/50831/223-234.pdf.

Use/limits: Use supported architectural descriptions and cite locations of travelers accurately. Reject broad public-access claims, speculative 400 BCE chronology and wording suggesting summer pond freezing; compare with Jørgensen and physics research.

## SRC04 — Hareth Pochee — The Physics of Freezing at the Iranian Yakhchal

https://www.maxfordham.com/practice-people/journal/the-physics-of-freezing-at-the-iranian-yakhchal

Read: 2026-10-09: full research-team explanation, including storage, radiation, evaporation and vent hypothesis. Published plan and storage-model section visually inspected 2026-10-09; north-side pond and below-ground store verified. Model dimensions/insulation thickness are not assigned to our reconstruction.

Use/limits: Same research family as SRC02, not an independent replication. Useful explanatory summary; alternative vent interpretation is a hypothesis.

## SRC05 — Layla Diba — Clothing in the Safavid and Qajar periods

https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/clothing-x/

Read: 2026-10-09: complete Qajar section, especially later nineteenth-century distinctions, traditional robes and trousers, court/ordinary differences.

Use/limits: Costume source only. Select subdued ordinary layers; do not borrow elaborate early-Qajar court portraits as everyday workwear.

## SRC06 — British Museum — Sykes.127, Iranian men and a boy, c.1900

https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/AA_Sykes-127

Read: Catalogue and visible photograph inspected 2026-10-09; plain layered garments and trousers.

Use/limits: Clothing comparison only, not a Meybod location or ice-house worker. Black-and-white image does not establish garment colours.
