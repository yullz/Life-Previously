## 5. Lunch at the counter

::: S010 | Server lifts a plain small serving bowl behind counter; no labelled menu or invented dish close-up. [same-as: S008] [pan-left]

## 3. The floor is warm
::: S041 | Same simple warm room with tiled floor occupying lower left, plaster walls intact. [pose: closeup-smile 0.8,1.02 0.82] [note: Roman Baths • western heated rooms] [same-as: S040] [zoom-in]
We are visiting the fourth-century bathing complex. Its heated rooms give a very literal version of comfort under your feet.

::: S042 | Section through the warm room with the floor slab lifted at left to show rows of short square tile stacks beneath; nobody present; says reconstruction. [same-as: S040] [pan-left]
Lift away the floor. A forest of short supports appears underneath.

::: S043 | Same section, muted arrows from side furnace through open spaces between stacks. [still]
These stacks hold the floor above a cavity. Heat from a furnace travels through that space beneath the occupied room.

::: S044 | Close on preserved square tile stacks under a missing floor, museum-plain; no people. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S040] [pan-right]
The Roman Baths preserve examples of these tile supports. They are heating evidence, not broken furniture.

::: S045 | Hypocaust section adds upward wall flue; keep hot-gas circuit separate from occupied air. [still]
The system is called a hypocaust. At Bath, heated air circulated beneath floors and through parts of the walls too.

::: S046 | Warm room floor and bench at left; clear right. [pose: eyebrow-raise 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S040] [still]
These are passages for hot gases, not modern water pipes zigzagging through concrete.

::: S047 | Furnace masonry edge and robust floor-support section, no handwritten page. [note: Vitruvius • Architecture V.10] [zoom-out]
The Roman writer Vitruvius describes building suspended hot-room floors on brick pillars. Someone had worked this problem through carefully.

::: S048 | One unnamed attendant places split wood beside low furnace mouth; normal muted tunic, no invented chains. [pose: watch-arms-crossed 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S047] [pan-right]
For somebody enjoying the warm room, the useful part stays out of sight. For somebody tending the furnace, it is the whole job.

::: S049 | Plain stack of dry wood and contained furnace glow; no name badge or written roster. [same-as: S047] [zoom-in]
We do not know the attendant's name or circumstances. The fuel and the furnace are the point.

::: S050 | Service area with fuel, furnace and attendant; clear safe working space, no impossible smoke entering warm room. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S047] [pan-left]
The heat has to come from somewhere. Your comfortable floor depends on a service space, a working fire and somebody keeping it going.

::: S051 | Two separate paths: spring to bathing pool, furnace to room floor. Distinct labels and no connecting arrow. [still]
Another trap at Bath: the Great Bath received naturally hot spring water. Do not turn that spring into a furnace product.

::: S052 | Return to intact warm-room floor and bench, no pool added. [pose: look-around 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S040] [pan-right]
Two sources of warmth can share one complex without being the same mechanism. Our countdown is about the heated rooms.

::: S053 | Same heated room framed through masonry entrance, no multiple houses or ubiquitous heating map. [pose: watch-arms-crossed 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S040] [zoom-in]
Nor does this substantial bathing complex prove that every Roman household enjoyed heated floors.

::: S054 | Warm room calm and uncluttered at left; clear right. [pose: closeup-smile 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S040] [still]
Your floor passes the comfort test. The effort is concentrated somewhere you would rather not stand.

::: S055 | Three masonry tokens on left, two book-design tokens right; heading fits safely. [still]
So far we have walked through buildings. The final two ideas take us into a different kind of evidence: an ancient engineering book.

::: S056 | Simple bronze chest with top slot, low left outlet and shallow bowl; no water flowing yet. [pose: eyebrow-raise 0.8,0.94 0.6] [note: Hero, Pneumatics 21 • described design] [pan-right]

## 2. The coin is also the switch

::: S058 | Close view of the plain bronze chest and spout; no book, no text. [note: Hero of Alexandria • Roman era] [same-as: S056] [pan-left]
Hero worked in Roman-era Alexandria. His biography is uncertain, so we invent no installation date.

::: S059 | Section 21 closed-state diagram: reservoir, sealed outlet box, suspended lid and lever plate. [still]
Inside the chest is a water reservoir. A small lid closes the outlet until the mechanism lifts it.

::: S060 | One blank ancient coin resting at the top slot of the bronze chest, close; no text. [note: Hero, Pneumatics 21 • reconstruction] [same-as: S056] [pan-right]
In Hero's design, the coin does two jobs: payment, and the weight that moves a lever.

::: S061 | Lever pivot between coin plate and lid; highlight the two ends without changing position. Use static successive states; no animated version is assumed by the native renderer. [still]
One end of the lever carries a plate. The other end holds the lid that closes the outlet.

::: S062 | A customer's hand drops a blank coin into the top slot of the chest at left; clear right. [pose: count-fingers 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S056] [still]
Drop the coin in. Inside, that end of the lever goes down, and the lid at the other end rises.

::: S063 | Valve detail from same diagram with clear gap between lid and outlet box, no floating disconnected rod. [still]
The moving part is small, but it changes the whole machine. The blocked outlet is now open.

::: S064 | Chest exterior at left with a thin stream from the spout into the shallow bowl; nobody touching it; clear right. [pose: thumbs-up 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S056] [pan-right]
Water flows through the pipe and out of the chest. No attendant needs to lift that lid for you.

::: S065 | Coin drops into separate dry collection space; plate rises, lid seats; stream stops. [still]
When the coin slides off the plate, the balance changes again. The lid drops and closes the outlet.

::: S066 | Closed exterior after operation; small amount of water in bowl, no stream. [same-as: S056] [pan-left]
The water stops. Your payment briefly changed the balance, then disappeared into the chest.

::: S067 | Chest at left; clear right. [pose: closeup-smile 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S056] [still]
It is a wonderfully direct arrangement. The coin does not send a message; it physically pushes part of the device.

::: S068 | Simple chest in restrained temple-context niche; no other machines, snacks or invented queue. [pose: watch-arms-crossed 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S056] [pan-right]

::: S069 | One reconstructed water chest in a simple alcove, no duplicated machines or crowd. [note: Described mechanism • extent of use uncertain] [same-as: S056] [zoom-in]
A description does not show how widely a device was installed. We cannot give every temple one.

::: S070 | Exterior chest and empty plain refill jug nearby; no person or claim of automated refill. [same-as: S056] [pan-left]
The vessel must also contain water. However clever the lever, an empty reservoir cannot provide your next serving.

::: S071 | One text-design icon, no multiplying device icons; clear qualification. [still]

::: S072 | Coin slot and bowl at left; clear right. [pose: eyebrow-raise 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S056] [still]
