## Who controls your day?
::: S013 | Three restrained human silhouettes with differentiated labels, no claim of shares. [still]
Some gladiators were enslaved. Some were condemned prisoners. Others enlisted as free or freed men, though their legal positions differed.

::: S014 | Two unrelated adult trainees waiting in passage, distinct faces, neither labelled by appearance. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S003] [pan-left]
A willing recruit and a captive might train together without having made anything like the same choice.

::: S015 | New trainee's sober face in profile at left; clear right. [pose: closeup-worried 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S003] [zoom-out]
You can ask whether this looks like a good career. Many people inside never had that question put to them.

::: S016 | Closed gate leaf and the attendant at left; clear right. [pose: eyebrow-raise 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S003] [still]
Even agreeing to fight did not mean retaining an ordinary customer's right to leave when the arrangement became unpleasant.

::: S017 | Three minimal nonviolent symbols: cord, flame outline, sheathed blade; no injured body. [note: Seneca, Moral Letters 37 • first century AD] [still]
The Roman writer Seneca describes men hiring out their strength for the arena, bound to endure restraint, burning, and the sword.

::: S018 | Closed gate leaf and plain wooden latch at left; clear right. [same-as: S003] [pan-left]
He uses that obligation in a moral argument. It is not a signed contract from this school.

::: S019 | Attendant closes one gate leaf in ordinary manner; trainee already inside, no force shown. [same-as: S003] [zoom-out]
But it makes the stakes clear. A volunteer could agree to a future that would be very difficult to take back.

::: S020 | Older plain-clothed manager beside instructor; trainee at post, one worker carrying a covered basket. [pose: look-around 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S001] [pan-right]
The school manager was called a lanista. Around him were instructors and other workers who kept the institution functioning.

::: S021 | Instructor checks practice shield edge while trainee waits; no written daily schedule. [same-as: S001] [zoom-in]
Your training was somebody's responsibility. So were the spaces and equipment that shaped each day.

::: S022 | Trainee stands stiffly before modest equipment bench, uncertain expression; no comedy humiliation. [pose: shrug-smirk 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S001] [pan-left]
Picture arriving full of confidence and discovering that confidence is the only piece of equipment you brought with you today.

::: S023 | Instructor indicates stance with open hand while trainee's wooden weapon points safely down. [pose: closeup-listening 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S001] [zoom-out]
You still need to learn how to stand, handle your weapons, and listen when somebody corrects you.

::: S024 | Plain bench with wooden practice sword and small shield; distinct equipment examples reserved for later graphics. [pose: look-around 0.8,0.94 0.6] [pan-right]
That learning depends on the kind of fighter you are becoming. There was no single bag of equipment labelled gladiator.

## The equipment changes the problem
::: S025 | Single secutor kit on plain stand, rounded helmet two small eye holes, large shield, short sword, arm protection. [note: Comparative equipment • Roman Empire, 1st–3rd centuries AD] [zoom-in]
Imagine being shown two different sets of fighting equipment. One includes a large shield and a helmet enclosing the head.

::: S026 | Large shield and plain secutor helmet, no plumes or legionary crest. [pose: closeup-listening 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S025] [pan-left]
This is a secutor's equipment. The shield gives substantial cover, while the helmet places a barrier between your face and the world.

::: S027 | Separate retiarius display: folded weighted net, three-pronged trident, dagger, left-arm sleeve and shoulder plate; no helmet. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [zoom-out]
A retiarius carries a net and trident, with arm and shoulder protection, but no helmet.

::: S028 | Two clear kit silhouettes, large shield versus net, with no numerical advantage score. [still]
Different equipment means different problems. Swap the kit around, and the same movements may no longer work.

::: S029 | Instructor demonstrates shield angle beside attentive trainee; wooden blade stays away from bodies. [pose: watch-arms-crossed 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S001] [zoom-in]
Instructors specialized too. Learning a particular fighting style meant more than finding somebody strong enough to swing a sword at you.

::: S030 | Single blunt wooden short sword and small practice shield on bench, no sharp metal blade. [same-as: S024] [pan-left]
For our first lesson, keep things simpler: a practice weapon, a shield, and a stationary target.

::: S031 | Trainee hand grips wooden handle, thumb and fingers anatomically coherent, weapon pointed down. [same-as: S024] [zoom-out]
The glamorous helmet can wait. Looking impressive is a poor substitute for knowing what your hands are doing.

::: S032 | Return to same post and instructor positions with trainee nearer, coherent court geometry. [pose: glance-nervous 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S001] [pan-right]

::: S033 | Post alone dominates left, trainee in soft background, no face painted on post. [same-as: S001] [zoom-in]
A wooden post sounds like a very undemanding opponent. It will not feint, chase you, or take advantage of a mistake.

::: S034 | Trainee in balanced stance with wooden sword beside post, no dangerous swing crossing instructor. [pose: watch-arms-crossed 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S001] [pan-left]

## The opponent who never gets tired
::: S035 | Source card: book and chapter, a plain post icon, a wicker shield and wooden sword icon; no facsimile. [still]
This is more than a convenient reconstruction. A Roman military handbook, Vegetius, describes recruits trained at a post with wicker shields and wooden swords of double weight.

::: S036 | Trainee holds the blunt wooden sword and small practice shield beside the post, instructor adjusting his grip at left; clear right. [pose: closeup-listening 0.8,1.02 0.82] [note: Vegetius, Epitoma rei militaris I.11 • 4th century AD] [same-as: S001] [pan-right]
He adds that the post is of the greatest use not only to soldiers but also to gladiators. Our post has good company.

::: S037 | Simplified courtyard circle and central possible post position; dashed interpretation symbol. [still]
At Carnuntum, researchers also identified a feature that might have held a post in the circular training area they reconstructed.

::: S038 | Trainee pauses with shield low, instructor points to shield rather than shouting. [pose: eyebrow-raise 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S001] [pan-left]
Our trainee's first attempt looks energetic. The instructor, however, has noticed something you missed.

::: S039 | Practice shield lowered beside torso, wooden sword held safely still; no contact or wound. [same-as: S001] [zoom-out]
Your weapon moved, but so did your protection. In this imagined exercise, correcting one movement means paying attention to the other.

::: S040 | Instructor guides shield height through gesture at left, trainee listens; clear right. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S001] [pan-right]
Less thrilling than winning a duel, perhaps. But a better reason to begin with wood.

::: S041 | Blunt rounded wooden edge shown clearly against straight grain; no sharp point. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S024] [zoom-in]
Wooden practice weapons reduced the danger of unnecessary injuries. They did not remove the need for discipline or turn training into play.

::: S042 | Four simple body-position icons, no prescriptive combat diagram or exact technique. [still]
Think how much an unfamiliar movement demands: feet, hands, balance, and where your attention goes.

::: S043 | Same court, trainee resets stance without a new weapon or outfit, instructor watches. [pose: scratch-head 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S001] [zoom-out]
Our trainee begins again. The post is still there. The instructor is still there. The excuse that you were surprised is disappearing.

::: S044 | Repeated scuffs in sand near post, no tick marks or number tally. [same-as: S001] [pan-right]
We cannot count repetitions from a first-day register. Our exercise illustrates practice without inventing a precise schedule.

::: S045 | Trainee lowers wooden sword while glancing toward distant gate, no public crowd. [same-as: S001] [zoom-in]
Nor should we pretend one successful exercise meant you were ready for a public fight. Learning and performing are different demands.

::: S046 | Instructor view of trainee's reset stance, post and small shield clearly separated. [same-as: S001] [pan-left]
To the new arrival, the arena might feel like the whole point. To the instructor, the immediate task is making progress.

::: S047 | Several quiet work zones in same courtyard with only three adults, no bustling fantasy camp. [pose: look-around 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S001] [zoom-out]
Much of this world exists before a crowd ever sees a fighter's face.

::: S048 | Corridor entrance into small room, sleeping platform visible; late-afternoon light not yet dark. [pose: look-around 0.8,0.94 0.6] [pan-right]
And when the practice stops, you do not necessarily leave the institution behind. The next doorway leads towards the living quarters.

## What the baths do—and do not—mean
::: S061 | Return to same basin and ledge, restrained light; no newly lavish mosaic. [pose: closeup-listening 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S008] [zoom-in]
Then there are the bathing facilities. Rooms, basins, heating and drains together support the interpretation of a bath suite within the school.

::: S062 | Basin in the left foreground, plain corridor beyond; clear right. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S008] [pan-left]
The next question is obvious: if fighters were treated as disposable, why provide facilities that helped maintain them in the first place?

::: S063 | Two linked simple symbols, bath basin and controlled gateway, equal visual weight. [still]
People can invest in somebody's body while denying that person control over its future.

::: S064 | Water surface reflecting plain window, narrow doorway behind; no luxury fantasy. [same-as: S008] [pan-right]
That is where our first day becomes harder to judge. A comfortable moment does not tell you who owns the next decision.

::: S065 | Unoccupied bath suite with modest ledge and shallow pool, no crowded nude scene. [pose: closeup-listening 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S008] [zoom-in]
The bath area is a useful correction to a simple picture of gladiator life as nothing except fighting and confinement.

::: S066 | Same controlled gate from inner passage, attendant by threshold without threatening pose. [pose: eyebrow-raise 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S003] [pan-left]
It also does not turn the school into a modern sports club. The entrance, the training, and the fighter's status still matter.

::: S067 | Manager watches practice from side, trainee focused; no bidding placards or coin pile. [same-as: S001] [zoom-out]

## What happens when the crowd arrives?
::: S075 | Empty civilian arena from ground level, low provincial tiers, doorway behind at left; clear right. [pose: look-around 0.8,0.94 0.6] [zoom-out]
What we can follow is the purpose towards which the training points. Beyond this compound stands the public amphitheatre.

::: S076 | Arena sand in foreground, sparse distant spectator silhouettes added only here, no combat. [pose: look-around 0.8,0.94 0.6] [same-as: S075] [pan-right]
And that is a different kind of space. The same skills will eventually be tested where other people have come to watch.

::: S077 | Entrance tunnel opens to sand, two distant adult fighters wait separately with lowered kit, no violence. [pose: closeup-worried 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S075] [zoom-in]
A trained fighter could be hired for a public show. The school prepared people for performances that might wound or kill them.

::: S078 | Simple two-outcome branching without percentages or guaranteed branch. [still]
But losing did not always mean dying. Roman evidence allows for a defeated gladiator to receive mercy and leave the arena alive.

::: S079 | Arena sand with a lowered wooden sword at left, no fighters; clear right. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [note: Seneca, Moral Letters 37 • first century AD] [same-as: S075] [zoom-out]
Seneca mentions a fighter lowering his weapon and appealing to the people. That possibility is very different from a promise of safety.

::: S080 | Plain empty patch of arena and lowered weapon at edge, no numeric odds. [pose: closeup-thoughtful 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S075] [pan-right]
We cannot give our imagined arrival reliable survival odds. A reassuring number would conceal too many unknowns.

::: S081 | Two fighters separated by space, one has weapon lowered; no executioner or clear verdict. [same-as: S075] [zoom-in]
The important correction is narrower: the institution invested in trained fighters, and a public defeat did not have only one possible ending.

::: S082 | View from doorway toward distant opposing fighter, blades lowered and no contact. [same-as: S075] [pan-left]
That still leaves a dangerous future. Knowing mercy existed would not make standing in front of an armed opponent feel ordinary.

::: S083 | Locally drawn source card: pot outline with the two names as captions; no counterfeit inscription. [note: Colchester Vase • Roman Britain] [still]
Even recognition was a complicated reward. A pot from Roman Colchester names two fighters, Memnon and Valentinus: a souvenir of one show.

::: S084 | Distant audience silhouettes above solitary fighter, no triumphal hero glow. [pose: closeup-listening 0.8,1.02 0.82] [same-as: S075] [pan-right]
Being recognizable did not by itself mean being free. Admiration from spectators and control over your own life are different things.

## The real first-day shock
