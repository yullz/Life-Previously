# Research and boundaries

The hook is an imagined first day whose first opponent is a wooden post. Route: controlled gate, status, equipment and practice, sleeping rooms, heated hall/baths, public arena. c.AD 200 is a broad Imperial narrative setting, not the date of a preserved diary or exact construction phase. Carnuntum supplies spatial constraints; museum objects and Seneca supply separately labelled comparative evidence. Original research compared the current site account's qualified wording with the more confident 2014 paper and VIAS summary. Keep the more careful wording. Read the underlying lamp record: the correct training lamp is 74.51.2030, not the different gallery link's74.51.1845. The latter depicts defeated combatants, so using it as training proof would be wrong. Reject Ephesus ash-drink/vegetarian headlines as a local meal plan; reject exact 75 occupants, medical room and underground punishment cell as certainties. Heated hall can be assembly or training. No staged death, first-day duel or guaranteed survival. The story's emotional shift is from fear of an opponent to recognition of institutional control. Original illustrations, not copied research reconstructions; room plans establish geography, elevations remain illustrative.

## SRC01 — Neubauer et al. — The discovery of the school of gladiators at Carnuntum, Austria (Antiquity 88,2014,173–190)

https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/4ACC29C5CC928A88A8A4F5ADC3E989CB/S0003598X00050298a.pdf/div-class-title-the-discovery-of-the-school-of-gladiators-at-carnuntum-austria-div.pdf

Read: 2026-10-09 (Astra); 2026-10-10 re-read in full (PDF downloaded and parsed): main complex 2800 m² within a walled plot of about 11,000 m² west of the municipal amphitheatre; 'A single and easily controlled entrance into the complex can be seen on its eastern side, facing the amphitheatre … 2.4m-wide gate'; walk of about 80 m to the amphitheatre's north gate; 'a free-standing circular structure 19m in diameter, which could be interpreted as the training arena … surrounded by wooden spectator stands set on stone foundations'; 'Evidence for a feature resembling a post-hole … in the centre of the arena. This might be the foundation of the palus'; 'The division of the gladiators by the use of pali facilitated training and instruction specific to the type of armament'; instructors 'probably were drawn from the ranks of senior and ex-gladiators'; 'the administrative block and living quarters of the school's owner, the lanista … head of his familia gladiatoria with complete legal power over the life and death of the gladiators—unless they were Roman citizens'; 'a central row of small cells 3–7m² in area'; 'an estimated total of 75 gladiators could have been housed' (an assumption-based estimate); a sunken cell 'probably used for disciplinary measures'; hypocaust and 6.5 m² praefurnium: 'we interpret this building either as a heated training room or an assembly hall'; bath complex with pool, heated floor, apse, well, water line from the aqueduct, sewers and latrines; pottery 'generally date to the second and third centuries AD'.

Use/limits: Non-invasive interpretation; room functions and capacities have different confidence. Do not use an exact 75 residents, daily massage, definite punishment cell, normal winter -25C, or inferred medical room as established local facts. Later 2014 excavation report exists but accessible content was paywalled; not used.

## SRC08 — Archaeology Magazine — Weapons of the Ancient World: Gladiator Weapons (May/June 2020)

https://archaeology.org/issues/may-june-2020/collection/gladiator-weapons/weapons-of-the-ancient-world/

Read: 2026-10-10: 'the Romans boosted their entertainment value by pitting gladiators who specialized in different sets of weapons, armor, and fighting style against each other. A secutor often opposed a retiarius, who was equipped with a net in one hand and a long trident in the other.'

Use/limits: A magazine overview of wider Roman evidence, not Carnuntum's inventory.

## SRC10 — Vegetius — Epitoma rei militaris, Book I chapter 11 (Latin text, The Latin Library)

https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/vegetius1.html

Read: 2026-10-10: 'Quemadmodum ad scuta uiminea uel ad palos antiqui exercebant tirones'; wicker shields and 'clauas ligneas dupli aeque ponderis pro gladiis'; recruits exercised at the post morning and afternoon, attacking it 'tamquam contra aduersarium'; 'Palorum enim usus non solum militibus sed etiam gladiatoribus plurimum prodest'.

Use/limits: A fourth-century military handbook describing an old practice; it confirms post training with wooden weapons for gladiators in general, not a Carnuntum timetable.

## SRC11 — Davis, Pearce, Carroll, Moore, Nowell and Montgomery — Gladiators at Roman Colchester: re-interpreting the Colchester Vase, Britannia 55 (2024)

https://doi.org/10.1017/S0068113X24000187

Read: 2026-10-10: abstract and inscription section: 'The locally made colour-coated ware vessel known as the Colchester Vase is argued to be a commissioned piece recording a performance in the town'; captions 'MEMNON SAC VIIII VALENTINV LEGIONIS XXX' labelling 'the secutor and retiarius'; 'One name, Memnon, is argued to be a stage name'; 'The connection of another combatant, Valentinus, to the 30th legion is re-considered'; the single names 'raise the question as to whether they precisely identify the status of the performers, for example as slaves or peregrini … or simply supply the stage names'; keyword 'souvenir'; the vessel's final use as a cremation urn.

Use/limits: Roman Britain, not Carnuntum; the fighters' legal status is left open by the authors, and the script does so too.
